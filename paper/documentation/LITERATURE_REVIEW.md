# Literature review: where the paper sits in the field (2026-09-08; the ⚠ rows verified at the sources and our rows replaced by the champion night's on 2026-09-09 — `Report_2026-09-09_Champion_Verdict.md` §3)

Purpose: orient the paper honestly. Every number below carries the protocol under which it was reported (training set, steps, restarts, selection, test set), because on Sudoku-Extreme the protocol moves the number by more than the differences between models. ⚠ marks numbers read from abstracts or secondary sources that must be verified in the full text before they enter a table. Our own numbers are the champion night's (2026-09-09): the seed triple, and the one-seed w192 and set-attention arms, labeled.

## 1. The columns that make Sudoku-Extreme numbers comparable

1. **Training set.** The HRM/TRM convention trains on 1,000 puzzles from the 3.8M-puzzle training split (with position and digit augmentation) and tests on the 422,786-puzzle test split. A second family trains on the full split (2.7M to 3.8M puzzles). The two are different problems: the first is a small-data test of inductive bias, the second is a large-data test of the solver. They must never share a row without the column.
2. **Restarts and selection.** Single pass (one deterministic rollout) · single stochastic rollout (noise at inference, no selection) · k restarts with a verifier (coverage) · k restarts with a learned or residual selector, no verifier · k restarts with a vote.
3. **Steps.** The number of outer iterations at inference (16, 64, 128, 1,024, 10,000). Depth changes the number by 6 to 10 points on decimating decoders (our depth law).
4. **Search.** Some systems add branching, backtracking, hypothesis pinning or a symbolic solver at inference; their 100 % rows are search results, not model outputs.
5. **Test set.** The full 422,786; a 25,000-puzzle frozen subset (Sotaku); 300 puzzles (LDT); our labeled 100,000 (D64) / 20,000 (D128) / 5,000 (scans) subsamples.
6. **Weights and numerics.** EMA vs raw; bf16 vs fp32 (our cross-route check shows ≤ 0.13 pp on rates with a ≈ 5 % per-puzzle disagreement floor from chaotic amplification of round-off).

## 3. What the table says about our position (honest)

1. **Under the thousand-puzzle convention, single pass, the DEC leads every published number we know of, with seeds:** the triple 95.02 ± 0.65 at D16 (full test; its worst seed 94.34) and 98.18 ± 0.61 at D64 (100k), the w192 arm 95.91 / 99.10, against FPRM 94.2 (pass@1, 7M, adaptive halting), SE-RRM 93.73, CMM 93.7, EqR 93.0 / 93.15 at D64, TRM 87.4; on identical puzzles through one evaluator the triple is +8.5 / +5.1 / +3.7 / +3.1 pp over EqR's released weights at D16 / D64 / D128 / D256. This is the column the abstract's numbers live in and the abstract must say "single pass" and "the thousand-puzzle convention".
2. **On the restart columns we are at or above the field's headlines, on a subsample, labeled:** the w192 arm reads 99.88 residual-selected = verified at k = 128 and 99.82 at k = 32 (5,000-puzzle subsample, t64), the triple 99.28–99.70 at k = 32; EqR reports 99.8 with 128 restarts and residual selection (98.85 / 98.89 at k = 128 on the 20k through our evaluator); PTRM 98.75 with 100; GRAM 97.0 with 20 votes; guided reasoning 98.0. The honest sentence: at or above EqR's headline at the same k on a different 5,000-puzzle subsample (binomial ± 0.05–0.15 pp), one seed for the w192 arm; the k = 128 rows for C1–C4 are the filler's owed rows.
3. **Above every restart number sit the single-stochastic-rollout solvers trained on the full split, and one thousand-puzzle solver read as a peak over compute:** the diffusion curriculum's 99.90 (≈ 3.83M training puzzles, 10,000 steps) and Sotaku's 99.12 (2.7M puzzles, 1,024 steps, 25k test subset) are a different training regime, separated by the column. FRM's 99.5 IS on the thousand-puzzle convention (7.0M, three seeds) but on a 1,000-puzzle test subset (± 0.2 pp) and as a peak over an accuracy–compute curve; our nearest rows are the w192 arm's 99.42 (D128, 20k), 99.60 (D256, 5k) and 99.88 (k = 128) on different subsets — within each other's error; the paper says so and does not claim the absolute frontier. Reading Sotaku (open) through the suite is the natural next field row.
4. **The 100 % rows are search results** (LDT's lattice with branching on 300 puzzles; the hypothesis-pinning blog): they belong in the table with the search column filled, as the verification-game ceiling that a verifier plus search reaches, which is exactly the D3 point of the Sudoku-vs-ARC section (a verifier makes coverage a search problem).
5. **The "field's best is 99.8" question, answered:** 99.8 is EqR's multi-restart column (D64, 128 restarts, residual selection); its single-pass D64 number is 93.0. Our 98.1 is single pass. Same model, two columns; the paper's table carries both for every system, and no sentence compares across columns without saying so.
6. **Parameter and compute framing.** The w384 DEC is 55 % of EqR's parameters at 4.6× the arithmetic per step; the w192 DEC is 16 % at 1.34× — at D64 it is +6.0 pp over EqR on identical puzzles (1.31 vs 0.97 TMAC per puzzle) and +3.2 over EqR's D256 at a third of the arithmetic (different subsamples). Sotaku's 797K and LDT's 800K are of the w192's size. "Efficiency" is now a per-parameter AND a near-matched-compute claim at w192; the abstract states the cost in the same sentence as the accuracy, with the MAC column labeled analytic until the instrument lands.

## 4. The mechanistic neighbors (what overlaps with our instruments, and how to position)

- **Fractal basins trap latent reasoning (Lai, Bao, Quinn, Gilpin; arXiv 2609.04963, 4 September 2026).** Reads EqR, FPRM, a looped LM (Parcae) and a looped transformer as dynamical systems on Sudoku-Extreme (with difficulty ratings), Maze, Countdown, ARC-AGI-1 and linear systems: basins measured by perturbing initial latents on random 2-D slices and timing convergence; basin entropy, boundary entropy, an uncertainty exponent, a fast Lyapunov indicator; the finding that trajectories are trapped near saddle points that are nearly-correct solutions ("grids with repeated digits" on Sudoku), that basin entropy rises with difficulty, and that solvability appears as a bifurcation in training. Overlap with ours: their perturbed-initial-latent basins are our init radius and multi-restart breadth; their saddle-point trapping is our stall with violations and our chaotic amplification of round-off on churning trajectories; their bifurcation is our trajectory law's onset. Difference: they measure basin geometry of released models; we measure the decoder class (commitment dynamics), the selector law, the calibration gap, the memorization trajectory, and the one-variable design decomposition, and we read the released weights and our own on identical puzzles. Cite as concurrent and complementary; adopt their basin-entropy vocabulary where it names the same object; the "nearly-correct saddle" is the physics reading of our failure-class continuum's Sudoku end.
- **Recursive Reasoning as Attractor Landscape Search (Efstathiou & Balwani; workshop on latent and implicit thinking, 2026).** Sparse-autoencoder analysis of TRM on Sudoku-Extreme: hypotheses form early, then feature activations settle into approximately periodic patterns; recursion as search through an attractor landscape. Overlap: our commitment-at-step-one and syndrome-oscillation instruments read the same behaviour without an autoencoder. Cite.
- **Equilibrium Reasoners (Huang, Geng, Kolter).** The attractor hypothesis and the depth/breadth scaling axes; their randomized-init training and Langevin noise are the mechanisms our selector law and init-distribution law explain (RI cleans the wrong fixed points; the noise is a training device whose released inference setting costs 10 points). Our field study reads their released weights.
- **FPRM (Movahedi et al.).** Pre-norm, residual scaling, fixed-point halting with damping; 94.2 single pass at 7M. The closest single-pass comparator; its convergence-residual halting is the same object as our residual selector. Read its weights through the suite if released.
- **PTRM, GRAM, guided reasoning, Speed is Confidence.** Four ways to turn stochastic trajectories into accuracy: a Q-head selector, a vote, halting-head reweighting, halt-first selection. Our verifier-dependence table is where they sit: the halting head is a verifier (AUC ≥ .998 on the released models), the vote is below cold on wide-funnel maps without a verifier, and residual selection works only after RI/NI. "Speed is Confidence" reports that 89 % of failures are selection problems with a 99 % ceiling; our selector law says when selection is free.
- **The diffusion curriculum and FRM.** Noise-driven single rollouts that explore and commit; trained on the full split. Their claim that a single stochastic rollout needs no restarts or verifier is the strongest competitor to the restart columns; our instruments' question for them is the decoder class and the calibration at stalls under fresh noise. Open weights, if any, are the next field-study rows.
- **SE-RRM and G-RRM.** Symbol equivariance is published (March 2026) and extended to guiding symbolic solvers; our contribution beside it is the decomposition on identical puzzles, the no-augmentation row, the equilibrium objectives on the loop, and the instruments. Their size generalization (9×9 → 16×16, 25×25) is an axis our fixed 81-cell mixer cannot attempt; state it in limitations.
- **The Lattice Deduction Transformer.** A learned elimination operator on a candidate-set lattice with soundness by construction plus search: the neuro-symbolic end of the decoder-class axis (our reference decoders peeling / BP / BP + decimation are its hand-designed cousins). Cite in the reference-decoder section and in the verification-game discussion.
- **Quantizing recursive reasoning models (Ingolfsson et al., ETH).** An independent reproduction of EqR at D16/32/64 (86.4 / 90.4 / 93.0) that matches ours; cite in the reproduction column.
- **TRM on ARC-AGI-1 (Roye-Azar et al.).** The 1,000-sample vote is worth ≈ 11 points, the puzzle identifier is load-bearing (zero accuracy without it), and most accuracy is reached at the first recursion step: the ARC-side facts our §6 needs (candidate diversity as the substitute for a verifier; one-shot solves). Cite.
- **The Confidence Shortcut (Kim & No).** Confidence-based decoding in masked diffusion models commits to locally easy digits before their long-range dependencies resolve, and confidence-aligned training amplifies the tail error tenfold; the same failure our calibrated-commitment gap measures on decimating decoders. Cite beside Law 2.

## 5. What this changes in the paper

1. The abstract's comparison names its column ("single pass", "the thousand-puzzle convention") and adds the restart column with its numbers (99.4 at k = 32 vs EqR's 99.8 at 128) and one sentence on the full-split solvers (99.9 with millions of training puzzles), so that no reader can think we claim the absolute frontier.
2. The results table (§4) carries every row of §2 with the six columns of §1; the appendix carries the reproduction distribution (TRM 71.9–87.4).
3. Related work (§7) gains the four mechanistic neighbors with the positioning sentences above; the fractal-basins paper is cited as concurrent (four days before this draft).
4. Limitations (§8) state: single seed until the triple; the D64 row on a subsample; the restart column at k = 32 only; no row against the full-split-trained solvers; no size generalization; the compute column.
5. Owed measurements that would strengthen the position at small cost: the k = 128 scans on C1–C4 (the filler's queue; C0 / C5 / C6 are banked) and the full-test D64 rows; the Sotaku checkpoint through the suite (a full-split single-pass solver's decoder class and calibration); FPRM's and FRM's weights if released; a seeded attention arm.
