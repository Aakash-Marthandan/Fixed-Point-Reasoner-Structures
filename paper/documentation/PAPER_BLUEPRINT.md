# Paper blueprint: a computer-science paper in the PI's voice, with hep-th depth

The target: a reader of the recursive-reasoning literature (HRM, TRM, EqR and their successors) finishes the paper knowing (i) what the physics is and why the renormalization-group (RG) framing is the right lens, (ii) exactly what each measurement tool measures and how to compute it on their own model, (iii) which regularities ("laws") hold on our arms, on the field's released weights and across the corpus, with the numbers in tables whose protocol columns make them reproducible, and (iv) that Sudoku and ARC, which the field evaluates as one benchmark family, are two regimes that the same instruments separate operationally. The presentation is that of a strong ML conference paper (EqR-like: crisp abstract with numbers, a Figure 1 that carries the idea, a method section with equations, experiments with protocol-labeled tables, one-variable rows, related work, limitations); the depth is that of the dissertation (definitions with meaning, statements with independent checks, conventions fixed once, speculation quarantined).

**Order is logical, not chronological (the PI, 2026-09-08).** The program ran ARC first and Sudoku second; the paper presents Sudoku first because that is where the instruments read cleanly and the numbers sit at the field's frontier, and then presents the ARC work as the section that shows what the instruments read on a generalization task and why the two regimes must be separated. The ARC material is written in retrospect: what the ARC era established (retention as the basin-existence probe, basin existence vs reachability vs depth, the reachability wall, the priced-code laws, the init-distribution law) is stated as settled prior work that the Sudoku results are read against, and the section closes as the prior-work reference for the ARC paper that follows.

## 1. The four things the paper must teach, in order

1. **The physics and the RG connection, operationally.** A recursive reasoner is an iterated map on a state; running it is a flow; a solved puzzle is a fixed point that the flow reaches from the puzzle's start; the loop's design decides whether the flow commits (decimates) or averages (soft); symmetry of the state decides what augmentation must buy; a price on the information crossing the map's internal cuts decides what the code spends on. The RG connection is stated as a correspondence table (physics object → object in the model → the instrument that measures it → the law observed), never as an unmeasured metaphor. Every physics word that appears in the paper appears in that table with an instrument beside it.
2. **The instruments, as definitions a reader can implement.** Each instrument gets: a numbered definition (what is computed, on which set, at which depth, with which weights), one sentence of meaning ("a value of x means the decoder has ..."), one line of a known reading (a grid from the corpus), and the law it feeds. The instruments are the paper's method; the DEC and the champion numbers are what the method found on Sudoku; the ARC readings are what the same method found on a generalization task.
3. **The laws, with their evidence and their scope.** Seven Sudoku laws (the two-axis decoder class set by the loop; the class-intrinsic calibrated-commitment gap; the selector law; the memorization/trajectory law; the depth law; exact symmetry replaces the orbit; init-invariance is trainable and recipe-dependent), each stated in one sentence inside a `law` environment with a table or a figure and its scope (cells, regime, sets) and mechanism reading; and the cross-domain laws of §6 (the landscape-class law; the verifier's worth; the failure-class continuum; the price's sign across domain and scale), stated with the same discipline.
4. **The two regimes, separated operationally.** Verification games (Sudoku: one rule, a free verifier, a per-instance difficulty axis, search-limited) and generalization tasks (ARC: a rule inventory, no verifier, transfer to unseen families, inventory-limited) are told apart by what the same instruments read on each, not by argument; the section says which instruments and mechanisms port and which are structural to one domain.

## 2. Section plan (main text ≈ 9 pages at the venue's limit; appendices carry the rest)

**Abstract.** Context in two sentences (the field's recursive reasoners; their numbers are protocol-dependent and their mechanisms unmeasured). What we build (the DEC). What we measure (an instrument suite; the field's released checkpoints through it). What we find on Sudoku (the laws, named). One sentence on ARC: the same instruments read the other regime the other way, and the paper says operationally what ports. At most three numbers, each with its protocol column in the same sentence.

**1. Introduction (≈ 1.25 pages).** Two facts, one inference. Fact one: the field's models commit most of the grid in one step and then propagate (the decimation picture). Fact two: their reported numbers move by more than the differences between them under conventions no paper reports. Inference: the object of study is the flow, and it needs instruments before it needs a new architecture. A third fact, stated and deferred to §6: the same field evaluates ARC with the same models and protocols, and on ARC the instruments read the opposite verdicts. Then the contributions as a numbered list, each one sentence, each pointing to a section and a table. Then Figure 1: the decoder-class ladder across the corpus on the two axes.

**2. Setup: the cells, the loop, the protocol (≈ 1 page).** The field's block and two-timescale loop (equations); our nine-field state and the exact S9 property; the training-regime column; the evaluation-protocol column; the one table "Protocol and regime columns" every later number cites. Conventions fixed here, once. The priced information flow (the flux ledger and the toll β) is defined here in one paragraph because §6 reads it on both domains.

**3. The RG lens and the instrument suite (≈ 1.75 pages).** 3.1 the correspondence table. 3.2 the instruments, grouped: record-level, checkpoint-level, trajectory-level, compute; each as a numbered definition with its meaning sentence. 3.3 the reference decoders (peeling, BP, BP + decimation) that anchor the class axis. Retention (handed the answer, does the map keep it) is defined here as the basin-existence probe because it is the instrument both domains share and the one the ARC section is built on.

**4. Results on Sudoku-Extreme (≈ 2.25 pages).** 4.1 the one-variable decomposition on identical puzzles (the field's recipe reproduced; the orbit removed; the exact symmetry added; the objectives; the seeds) with the headline table (protocol, regime, selection and compute columns; coverage columns labeled). 4.2 the champion recipe with n = 3 seeds and the depth ladder D16 / D64 / D128 / D256. 4.3 the laws these arms establish, each with its figure or table. 4.4 the mechanism arms (the orbit lever, the operator, the width, the commit head) as one-variable readings against the seed floor, with the calibrated-commitment gap as the open mechanism.

**5. The field's models through the same instruments (≈ 1 page).** The reproduction table (their torch numbers vs ours, cross-route agreement); the design-choice ledger in the bold run-in form (EMA, the Langevin noise, depth, the selection grid, the curriculum, the halting head, memorization); the corpus-wide figures that put natives, our cells and theirs on the same axes.

**6. Two problems in one boat: Sudoku and ARC through the same instruments (≈ 1.25 pages; the prior-work section for the ARC paper).** Opens with the field's convention (one architecture, one protocol, both benchmarks) and the claim that the instruments separate them. Then the ARC work in retrospect, as measured rows: what retention established there (the basin-existence probe; the repair of truth-erasure by the equilibrium core; basins exist for a minority of task pairs), the reachability wall (most converged limits confidently wrong; iteration, temperature and attempts do not help where no basin exists; basin-existence conditioning), the priced-code laws on ARC (the toll buys transfer to unseen rule families at a mild cold cost; the throat declines with capacity; the optimization-length tax on transfer), and the init-distribution law (randomized-init training pays exactly under the init distribution it trained on, on both domains). Then the differences, each a measured row with its instrument, in the bold run-in ledger: **the inventory vs the single rule** (codebook size exists on ARC only; endpoint statistics are its Sudoku shadow); **what generalization means** (unseen families vs harder instances); **verification** (the verifier's worth quantified; the conversion stack as its substitute); **what limits performance** (inventory-limited vs search-limited, the two-sided iteration result as the flagship figure, sharpened: the wall is cell- and loop-dependent, not a domain constant); **failure classes** (wrong-and-stable vs stuck propagation, quantified as one continuum by the converged-wrong rate, from ARC's 0.92 to the DEC's 0.0004, with the memorized Sudoku map acquiring the ARC texture); **information geometry** (the throat as a task-set constant vs an instance-conditional meter; the knee vs the UV profile); **adaptation** (the TTT layer exists on ARC only); **the price's sign** (transfer dividend on ARC; a kill, then a stability dividend, then the precondition for training the deep map on Sudoku, with precision and draw-diversity competing for the budget); **depth and breadth** (propagation and search on Sudoku; nothing and candidate diversity on ARC); **difficulty** (measurable per instance on Sudoku, not on ARC); **overtraining** (transfer erosion on ARC, stability and diversity loss on Sudoku, one pressure with two observables and weight decay as the measured lever). Throughout, the framing is forward-looking and scoped: the ARC readings were made on our early substrates (d16–d64, under 1M parameters) and describe the regime, not the task's solvability (the field's recursive reasoners solve ARC-1 in substantial part at 7M parameters; cited with protocol); the section says what the generalization task asks of the next model (inventory and selection) and what the instruments will read on it. Closes with the portability table (PORTED / ANALOG / STRUCTURAL-ARC / the back-port register: final-map retention, trajectory monitors and val-selection, the answer-mode contractivity, the explosion census, the decoder lens, the calibration lens) and the sentence that hands the reader to the ARC paper: which mechanisms of the field's loop are predicted to transfer to a generalization task and which cannot (the residual selector cannot; halting and the carry may), and the registered ARC-d96 rung with the back-ported suite that would settle it.

**7. Related work (≈ 0.5 page).** A narrative of results in the dissertation's manner: HRM, TRM, EqR, the successors (SE-RRM, PTRM, CGAR, CMM, GRAM, guided reasoning), the classical decoders. It ends at the gap: none measures the flow; the parity numbers are protocol conventions; symmetric states are concurrent work (SE-RRM); the two benchmarks are evaluated as one family.

**8. Limitations and future directions (≈ 0.4 page).** The honest ledger in the bold run-in form: the compute column; one verification domain at frontier accuracy and one generalization domain at a measured, sub-frontier accuracy; the calibrated-commitment gap measured, not closed; the selection instrument's resolution; the ARC scale-matching gap (Sudoku read to w384, ARC frozen at d64); what would settle each.

**9. Conclusion (≈ 0.2 page).** The one thesis sentence, then the one suggestive result.

**Appendices.** A. the cells and the loop in full (equations, parameter counts, the S9 proof). B. the instruments' exact computation (pseudocode, sets and seeds, the registered thresholds), including the ARC-era instruments (retention, the corruption ladder, codebook size and the packing plane, the flux ledger, the E1/E3 battery, the transfer gates). C. the registration and the decision rules of every campaign (the analyzer rules verbatim), the predictions and the scoreboard. D. the full tables (every arm, every row; the ARC ladder's seeded tables). E. the field-checkpoint port and the cross-route check. F. the reference decoders. G. compute accounting. H. the ARC-era conversion study (candidate supply × basin decoding; basin-preserving adaptation) as the reference for the ARC paper.

## 3. The RG connection: how to write it so it informs rather than decorates

- State the correspondence as a table with four columns: **physics** (a flow toward a fixed point; a basin of attraction; basin existence vs reachability; a spurious attractor; a decimation step; a relevant vs irrelevant perturbation; symmetry of the state; the price of information crossing a cut; critical slowing / a stall), **in the model** (the outer-step map on the carried state; the set of starts that reach the solution; whether a handed solution is kept vs whether a cold start finds it; a converged wrong grid; a cell committed and propagated; a design choice that moves the accuracy vs one inside the noise floor; the S9 action on the nine-field state; the flux ledger and the toll; the unsolved puzzle's late steps), **instrument** (exact-by-step and depth regressions; init radius and breadth; retention vs cold; the spurious rate and residual AUC; commitment at step 1 and the decimation-quality row; the one-variable decomposition against the seed floor; the exact-S9 test and the orbit arm; the throat and the knee profile; calibration at stalls and the syndrome oscillation), **law** (the depth law; Law 7; the landscape-class law; Law 3 and the failure-class continuum; Law 1; the decomposition table; Law 6; the price's sign arc; Law 2).
- Every physics sentence in the paper must be checkable against the table: if a sentence uses "attractor", the reader can find which instrument measured it. A physics word without an instrument is deleted.
- The RG framing earns its place in one paragraph of §3.1 that says what it PREDICTS (a decimating decoder gains from depth with no regressions; a soft one does not; a clean attractor structure makes residual selection verification-free; a map that holds no basin for a task cannot be helped by iteration) and then §4 and §6 show those predictions holding on our arms, on the field's weights, and on ARC. Predictions are the dissertation's independent check.
- Keep the dissertation's habit: one analogy sentence at most per section, precise and dropped immediately.

## 4. Tables and figures: the discipline

- Every table has the protocol columns (set, D, k, weights, numerics) and, for cross-system rows, the training-regime column; a caption says what is measured and where the number comes from (the analyzer script name in the appendix).
- Every cross-system number carries its protocol in the caption and, when it appears in prose, in the sentence.
- Coverage columns (verified@k, majority) are labeled as coverage; the headline is the single pass. The residual-selected column is verification-free and reported at the verifier's level where it reaches it.
- Figures: Fig. 1 the two-axis decoder-class ladder; Fig. 2 the commitment dynamics (soft vs decimating trajectories); Fig. 3 the selector law (spurious rate vs the residual AUC across 40 grids); Fig. 4 the depth ladder; Fig. 5 the trajectory law (monitor curves with the selected grids marked); Fig. 6 the field's design-choice ledger; Fig. 7 (the §6 flagship) the two-sided iteration result, extended inference on ARC vs Sudoku on one axis, same instrument, opposite verdicts; Fig. 8 the verifier's worth (verified vote vs unverified majority at k on the same draws) beside the failure-class continuum. Each figure caption is a paragraph that lets the figure stand alone.
- "record" appears only with the "program-" prefix; band names carry no comparator nicknames; every number comes from an analysis script, never typed by hand. The ARC numbers come from the ARC-era analyzers and verdict artifacts named in the ledger's archived entries, cited by file in Appendix D.

## 5. The EqR-like presentation, made explicit

EqR-like means: a Figure 1 that carries the mechanism; a method section whose equations a reader can implement; a table of Sudoku-Extreme results with columns for the protocol variants (single pass at D16 and D64; multi-draw with verification; residual selection) and rows for the comparators at matched protocol; ablations as one-variable rows against a stated floor; a short, specific related-work narrative; a limitations section that names costs. What we add from the dissertation: the definitions with meaning, the independent check for every claim (a reproduction row, a cross-route row, a registered prediction), the conventions fixed once, and the ledger of open questions written as questions with what would settle them. What we add beyond EqR's scope: the second domain read through the same instruments, presented as the thing that tells the two regimes apart.
