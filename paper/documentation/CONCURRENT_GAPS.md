# What the concurrent papers leave unexplained, and what our measurements say (2026-09-08)

Purpose: for each concurrent or closely related paper, the gap its authors state (quoted from the paper), what our instruments and laws read on that gap, how confident we are, the cheap measurement that would settle it, and the sentence the paper can carry. Confidence labels: **measured on their weights** (our evaluator on the released checkpoint) · **measured on ours** (our arms, identical puzzles) · **inferred** (a mechanism reading of measured rows) · **speculative** (a hypothesis we have not tested). Every number is from `Report_2026-09-08_Frontier_Analysis.md`, `Report_2026-09-07_NightA_Verdict.md`, `Report_2026-09-05_sportC2_Verdict.md` or the corpus tables unless stated. This file feeds §5 (the field's models), §7 (related work) and §8 (limitations); it is not compiled.

## 1. Fractal basins trap latent reasoning (Lai, Bao, Quinn, Gilpin; arXiv 2609.04963, 4 September 2026)

**Their gap 1.** "Why trajectories preferentially get trapped [near saddle points that are] grids with repeated digits ... remains mechanistically unclear."
**Our reading.** On every decimating decoder we measured, including EqR's released weights, the trap is the committed cell set, not a saddle of a potential. The model commits 91–99 % of cells after the first outer step at confidence 1.000; on the puzzles it fails, 43–72 % of those commitments are wrong, peeling from the committed cells contradicts the givens on 100 % of stalls, and the five most confident cells at a stall are right only 45–54 % of the time on the three public models. A "grid with repeated digits" is a stall whose confidently committed cells are wrong and whose propagation from them is locally self-consistent; the map has no revision mechanism because its confidence carries no information at stalls (Law 2, the calibrated-commitment gap). The trapped trajectory oscillates at high confidence (4.8–6.2 argmax flips per cell over the last 32 steps, syndrome oscillating by 18–29 on the DEC; 3.3 flips on the field cell), which is what a "saddle" looks like in the readout. **Confidence: measured on their weights (EqR, alphaXiv, CGAR) and on ours; the mechanism sentence is inferred.** **Settles it:** on the same EqR checkpoint, correlate their per-puzzle basin entropy with our per-puzzle stall-commitment fraction and confidently-wrong fraction; both are computable from released weights. **Sentence:** "The nearly-correct grids that trap the flow are stalls whose committed cells are confidently wrong; the trapping is a property of the decoder's commitment, which its confidence does not report."

**Their gap 2.** Basin entropy "strongly correlates with the number of iterations, a measure of overall task difficulty"; fractality "emerges during training" at a bifurcation; whether continued training could eliminate the trapping is not discussed.
**Our reading.** The trapping-time distribution is our exact-by-step curve: on the released models the fraction solved rises from D16 to D256 with zero regressions in 422,786 puzzles (alphaXiv 79.3 → 86.3, CGAR 86.1 → 94.1, EqR 86.5 → 95.9), with first-exact median 2–3 and p90 7–12; the tail that their basin entropy indexes is the tail of this distribution, and it is monotone in the puzzle rating (per-draw success geometric in rating). On training: more training does not remove the calibration gap in any regime we measured (18 decimating grids, ours and theirs, 41–62 % correct at confidence 1.000 at stalls), and past the peak it makes failures worse (memorized maps acquire near-valid confidently wrong grids, Law 4). Their bifurcation is, we expect, the onset of decimation: commitment at step 1 jumps from the soft regime (3–13 % of cells) to the decimating one (91–99 %); we have not yet measured commitment across training checkpoints. **Confidence: the depth and first-exact rows measured on their weights; the bifurcation reading is speculative until the C1 dynamics are computed on the 2k grids.** **Settles it:** the C1 row (commitment at step 1, confidently-wrong) on every banked 2k grid of the champion arms, at analysis time, $0. **Sentence:** "Their trapping times are the tail of the exact-by-step curve, which rises to 256 steps with no regressions; their bifurcation should be the step at which the decoder begins to commit."

**Their gap 3.** "the role of initialization noise" (they disable EqR's stochastic inference) is left open.
**Our reading.** Two measured laws: randomized-init training makes the map invariant to the start (EqR's init radius .99 of cold at ε = 3; alphaXiv .94; CGAR .80; HRM ≈ 0) and pays exactly on the init distribution it trained on (the init-distribution law); EqR's inference noise is a breadth device, not an accuracy device (noise .5 costs 10.1 pp at D16 per trajectory and raises verified coverage at k = 32 from 98.4 to 99.4). A basin measured under their protocol on an RI-trained model is wide by training. **Confidence: measured on their weights.** **Sentence:** "Initialization noise widens the basin only for models trained under it; at inference it trades per-trajectory accuracy for coverage."

## 2. Equilibrium Reasoners (Huang, Geng, Kolter; ICML 2026)

**Their gaps.** The residual-convergence signal "is not universally valid: for the baseline TRM, residual reduction can indicate convergence to spurious attractors" and becomes reliable only "after shaping the landscape"; the fraction of trajectories converging to wrong fixed points is not quantified; why randomized init and Langevin noise help is left at "consistent with the landscape modes"; the depth-before-breadth interaction is unexplained; λ and β are hand-tuned.
**Our reading.** The selector law quantifies the missing fraction with one instrument: among wrong draws, the fraction whose residual lies below the median residual of the correct draws (spurious rate) with its AUC and the ratio of residual-selected to verified accuracy: EqR 0.0 % (AUC .999, t1r 98.85 = verified 98.89 at k = 128), alphaXiv 2.6 %, CGAR 30.9 %, HRM 12–15 %; on the field's recipe reproduced on our stack one variable at a time, randomized-init training alone takes the spurious rate from 7.8 % to 0.0 (at a 1.4 pp coverage cost) while the anchor rows alone damage it (.52 with t1r 38); the RI-free selector is seed-fragile on both cells (7.8 vs 38 %; 4.9 vs 68.6 % with an AUC of .375, worse than chance). So "shaping the landscape" is measurable and its cause is the randomized-init rows. Two further measured rows they do not report: the EMA shadow is worth +16.8 pp on their released raw Langevin weights (the equilibrium is partly in the average), and their released inference noise of .5 costs 10.1 pp at D16. On depth-before-breadth: first-exact medians are 2–3 steps on the natural test distribution, so a residual read before step 4 is read on unconverged trajectories; breadth pays once residuals are informative. **Confidence: measured on their weights and on one-variable arms; the depth-before-breadth sentence is inferred.** **Sentence:** "Verification-free selection is a property of training, not of the cell: randomized-init rows remove the wrong fixed points, and the spurious rate says when the residual can be trusted."

## 3. Probabilistic Tiny Recursive Model (Sghaier, Parviz, Jolicoeur-Martineau; arXiv 2605.19943)

**Their gaps.** No fractions for the three trajectory classes (quick success, delayed success, bounded oscillation); "why some puzzles never escape" is not addressed; "problems that are harder to verify benefit less because the Q head may struggle."
**Our reading.** Their three classes are our record-level rows: quick success = first-exact 1–2 (the median on every decimating model), delayed success = the first-exact tail (p90 7–12, zero regressions to D256), failure = the stall with confident oscillation (2–9 % of puzzles at D64 on the field-class models). Why some never escape: at a stall the committed cells are wrong at confidence 1.000 and the map does not revise them (Law 2); the per-puzzle per-draw success rates are bimodal (43–57 % of puzzles never decodable on our soft natives against 33–43 % decodable on nearly every draw; the field's cell has 93.5 % of puzzles above a per-draw rate of .5), so "never escapes" is a property of the cell and its training, not of the puzzle alone. On the Q head: it is a verifier on Sudoku (AUC ≥ .998 on all four released models), which is why best-Q@100 sits within a point of pass@100; on a task without a checkable answer the same head has nothing to read (our §6). **Confidence: measured on the released TRM-class weights and on ours; PTRM's own weights are not released.** **Sentence:** "Their failures are confident oscillations at wrongly committed cells; the halting head selects well on Sudoku because it is a verifier there."

## 4. Fixed-Point Reasoners (Movahedi et al.; arXiv 2606.18206)

**Their gaps.** Whether the convergence residual predicts correctness "isn't directly tested"; no failure analysis (wrong fixed points vs oscillation); the halting threshold and the damping are chosen without a correctness criterion.
**Our reading.** The residual predicts correctness exactly when training has removed the wrong fixed points (AUC .999 on EqR; .375 on an RI-free DEC seed, where converged wrong draws are more converged than the correct ones); a halting rule on the residual inherits that condition. On the failure classes: on our soft natives, stalls are not fixed points (Jacobian ≈ 1, still moving) while solved endpoints are exact fixed points (λ .67–.73); on decimating decoders stalls are confident oscillations. Their pre-norm divergence at depth is the same phenomenon as our free-channel state explosion (unpriced attention flux composed through depth 12 at width kills training 4/4 while the priced twin trains 2/2). **Confidence: measured on ours and on the released TRM-class weights; FPRM's weights not read.** **Settles it:** FPRM's checkpoint through the suite if released (the spurious rate and the stall class). **Sentence:** "Residual halting is sound where the spurious rate is zero; the rate is measurable before the threshold is chosen."

## 5. Diffusion as a training curriculum (Drozdova et al.; arXiv 2609.01449, 1 September 2026)

**Their gaps.** Why constant maximal noise at inference beats annealing (99.90 vs 99.56) is unexplained; how "a single rollout both explores and commits" is asserted, not shown; no failure analysis of the 0.1 %; memorization with ≈ 3.83M training puzzles is not examined; no plateau analysis past 10,000 steps.
**Our reading.** (i) Fresh noise on the free cells with an uncorrupted carried memory is the field's per-pass noise and randomized-init training taken to their limit; the init-distribution law says a map trained under that noise is invariant to it and a map that was not is not, which is a candidate explanation for why the noise level that trained the map is the one that solves best at inference. (ii) "Explores and commits" is, in our vocabulary, a decimating decoder whose committed cells survive fresh noise on the free ones; whether the commitments are calibrated is the open question our calibration-at-stalls row would answer on their 0.1 % failures. (iii) Their protocol is a different problem from the thousand-puzzle convention: the memorization law (every recipe memorizes the thousand puzzles) has no purchase on 3.83M training puzzles, so their number and ours answer different questions, a large-data solver and a small-data inductive bias. (iv) Under fresh noise a solved grid can be knocked out, so their "the decoded output stabilizes" is a claim about depth regressions, which our exact-by-step instrument counts (zero on the deterministic released models to D256); whether it is zero under noise is the measurement. **Confidence: (i) inferred from the init-distribution law; (ii) and (iv) speculative until their model is read; (iii) measured (the memorization law on our arms and EqR's train-1k row).** **Settles it:** their checkpoint, if released, through `--record-by-step` under their noise, plus the stall-calibration row on the failures. **Sentence:** "A single noisy rollout that explores and commits is a decoder whose commitments survive the noise it was trained under; the instruments that would show it are the depth-regression count and the calibration at its failures."

## 6. Flow Reasoning Models (Helbling et al.; arXiv 2606.29150; v1 96.1 % on Sudoku-Extreme as an out-of-distribution benchmark at ≈ 30M parameters trained on another Sudoku dataset; the abstract's 99.5 % is version-dependent, verify)

**Their stated limitation.** "The view applies cleanly only where correctness is a checkable stable state; open-ended generation, settings without a clear fixed point, and larger scales are untested," with "high seed-to-seed variance."
**Our reading.** This is the verification-game / generalization-task distinction of our §6, stated by them as a caveat and by us as a measured separation: on ARC the answer is not checkable and the same instruments read inventory limits rather than search limits. Their seed variance is our noise-floor discipline (contrasts read only beyond twice a seed spread). **Confidence: measured (ours), inferred (theirs).** **Sentence:** "Where correctness is checkable the fixed point is a verifier; where it is not, the instruments read a different regime."

## 7. Generative Recursive Reasoning (Baek et al.; arXiv 2605.19376; 97.0 on a 1,000-puzzle test set with 20 votes)

**Their gap.** "Plausible but invalid generations may be mistaken for verified solutions."
**Our reading.** The unverified majority is not a verifier on Sudoku: at k = 256 it reads below the single pass on EqR and CGAR (88.1 vs 89.5; 84.4 vs 87.7) because wrong draws agree with each other, and on our wide-funnel natives the gap between verified and majority at k = 128 is 50–65 pp (68.8 vs 18.8; 70.7 vs 5.1); on the DEC arms the majority reads 93.6–96.7 against 100 verified at k = 256. A vote pays only where the map's wrong draws disagree, which is measurable per map (the unverified-vote row). **Confidence: measured on their competitors' weights and on ours; GRAM's weights not read.** **Sentence:** "A majority of stochastic trajectories is a verifier only for maps whose wrong draws disagree; on the released deterministic models they agree."

## 8. Guided reasoning (Corbett et al.; arXiv 2605.25230)

**Their diagnostics and gaps.** Local tube stability, guide alignment (the halting head as guide) and cloud-token entropy for abstention; the guide should become "a first-class design object"; broader evaluation open.
**Our reading.** Guide alignment is the halting head's AUC against exactness, which we measured at .998–.9999 on the four released Sudoku models (the guide is aligned there because it is a verifier); their Maze-Hard non-improvement is what that AUC predicts when it is low. Token entropy for abstention will fail on decimating decoders: readout entropy at step 1 is .007–.04 and the five most confident cells at a stall are right 45–54 % of the time at confidence 1.000, so the entropy of a wrong committed cell is indistinguishable from a right one (Law 2). **Confidence: measured on their base model class.** **Sentence:** "The guide works where the halting head is a verifier; entropy-based abstention cannot work where confidence carries no information at stalls."

## 9. Speed is Confidence (Dillon; arXiv 2601.19085)

**Their gaps.** "Why inference speed correlates with solution correctness" is unanswered; "89 % of baseline failures are selection problems" (1,820 of 2,037 solvable by some digit permutation), a "99 % ceiling", "217 irreducible failures require backtracking."
**Our reading.** Speed correlates with correctness on decimating decoders because a correct trajectory commits at step 1 and stays (monotone solves 69–77 % on the DEC, 59 % on the field cell, first-exact median 1–3) while a wrong one oscillates at high confidence; the halting head and the residual both read that difference (AUC ≥ .998). Halt-first selection across an ensemble is residual selection across members and inherits the selector law's condition. The "irreducible" set is cell-dependent: the DEC arms reach 99.5–99.9 % of the 5k scan set from some draw at k = 32 and 100 % of strat-512 at k = 256, and exact symmetry removes the digit-permutation axis their oracle exploits (a relabeling is the identity on the DEC). **Confidence: measured on ours and on the released TRM-class weights.** **Sentence:** "Speed is confidence because failures are oscillations, not slow convergence; the irreducible set shrinks with the cell."

## 10. Symbol-Equivariant RRMs and G-RRM (Freinschlag et al.; Bertram et al.)

**Their gaps.** Full-solve rate 0 % on 16×16 and 25×25 despite 52 / 31 % cell accuracy; complexity linear in the symbol count; trained with digit augmentation their equivariance makes redundant.
**Our reading.** The digit augmentation is redundant by measurement: on the equivariant DEC the digit orbit reads +3.2 pp at the selected grids, inside the corrected floor (A4 vs A3), while the same orbit is worth 65 pp to the field's cell. Their size collapse is consistent with a decimating decoder whose commitments are right cell-wise but whose propagation depth does not scale with the board (the depth law's gains are per doubling); untested by us. **Confidence: the augmentation row measured on ours; the size reading speculative.** **Sentence:** "On an equivariant cell the digit orbit buys nothing, measured; size generalization is the axis paper 2 must add."

## 11. The Confidence Shortcut (Kim & No; arXiv 2605.29123)

**Their gap.** "Sudoku represents a success case where confidence-alignment helps, suggesting task-dependency is crucial."
**Our reading.** The shortcut exists on Sudoku too, localized to the failures: decimating decoders commit locally easy cells at confidence 1.000 and are right on solved puzzles, but at stalls half of the committed cells are wrong, which is exactly "predicting locally easy digits before their long-range dependencies resolve" read at the failures rather than on average. Their stratification by structural difficulty is our per-rating spectroscopy. **Confidence: measured on ours and on the released weights.** **Sentence:** "The confidence shortcut on Sudoku is invisible in the average and complete at the stalls."

## 12. Lattice Deduction Transformers (Davis et al.; arXiv 2605.08605)

**Their stated limit.** 100 % on 300 Sudoku puzzles with a sound candidate-set lattice plus branching search; "roughly 36 % pass rate" on ARC-AGI "requiring additional machinery."
**Our reading.** Their lattice is an explicit, sound decimation with a verifier built in, the neuro-symbolic end of the decoder-class axis whose hand-designed cousins are our reference decoders (peeling, BP, BP + decimation); it is a search result, which the table's search column records. Its ARC plateau is what §6 predicts: with no verifier and an inventory limit, soundness has nothing to check against. **Confidence: inferred.** **Sentence:** "A verifier plus search closes the verification game; it is the generalization task that has nothing to check."

## 13. Tiny Recursive Models on ARC-AGI-1 (Roye-Azar et al.; arXiv 2512.11847) and the attractor-landscape reading of TRM (Efstathiou & Balwani, workshop 2026)

**Their observations.** On ARC most accuracy is reached at the first recursion step; the puzzle identifier is load-bearing (zero accuracy without it); the 1,000-sample vote is worth ≈ 11 points. On Sudoku, TRM forms hypotheses early and then settles into approximately periodic feature activations; recursion reads as search through an attractor landscape.
**Our reading.** Both are what our instruments read: one-shot solves on ARC (our horizon probe: +0 at t = 96) with the vote as the substitute for a missing verifier; on Sudoku, commitment at step 1 followed by confident oscillation at stalls (the periodic activations) — a decimating decoder does not search, it commits and propagates, and what looks like search is the oscillation of wrongly committed cells. **Confidence: measured on our substrates (ARC) and on the released TRM-class weights (Sudoku).**

Three framing gains beyond vocabulary:

1. **The stall is a transient, not a fixed point.** Our physics pass already read stuck states as still-moving (λ ≈ 1) and the decimating stalls as confident oscillations; the fractal-basins vocabulary lets the paper say this in one sentence with a literature behind it, and lets the correspondence table carry "transient chaos near a saddle" as the physics object beside "the stall" and "calibration at stalls".
2. **Contractivity has a theorem now.** FPRM's conditions turn our measured η drift and the explosion census into instances of a stated criterion; the paper can say what the learned damping must satisfy and show which recipes violate it.
3. **Where the noise enters is a design axis.** EqR (memory), PTRM (latent input), the diffusion curriculum (observable cells), guided reasoning (the inner recursion), TRM and the DEC (none): one column in the protocol table organizes the whole stochastic family, and our init-distribution law says which of them pays at inference.
