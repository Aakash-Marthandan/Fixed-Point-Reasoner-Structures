# Voice, style and tone markers (from the PI's dissertation, *Algebraic Structures of Entanglement*, CUNY 2026)

Purpose: every section of the paper is drafted and revised against this document so the paper reads in ONE voice, the PI's. The markers below were extracted from a close read of the abstract, the introduction (§1.1–1.4), the definitions and examples of chapter 2 (§2.1–2.2, §2.6), the technical narration of chapter 3 (§3.1–3.2), the derivation and physical interpretation of chapter 4 (§4.1–4.6), and the conclusions, extensions, future directions and closing remarks of chapter 5. Quoted phrases are the PI's own words and are the calibration set: when in doubt, write the sentence the way the dissertation would.

## 1. Who is speaking

- **"We", always, for the work.** The author is a collaborator writing for a colleague, never a salesman writing for a reviewer. "We propose", "we compute", "we show", "we expect", "we leave ... for future work". The reader is never addressed as "you"; the reader is carried along by "we".
- **Calm, declarative, scrupulously scoped.** Claims are stated as facts with their conditions attached in the same sentence. Nothing is oversold: the strongest adjectives in 184 pages are "crucial", "remarkable" (used once, and justified in the next clause: "The area law is remarkable because it relates a thermodynamic quantity to a geometric quantity"), "sharp" ("They provide a sharp statement of monogamy of entanglement in terms of group theory"), and "suggestive" ("Perhaps the most suggestive result is..."). Never "novel", "significantly", "state of the art", "impressive", "powerful", "we are the first".
- **Directness over formality when the two conflict.** The acknowledgments credit an advisor for teaching "directness and operational simplicity when it comes to the nature of frontier research", and the technical chapters practise it: "The remaining steps are straightforward." "This is a nice starting point since..." "We apologize for the notation, which we won't use after (3.15)." The voice admits what is awkward instead of hiding it.

## 2. Sentence-level markers

1. **Short declarative topic sentences that name what a thing IS.** "This is a statement about geometry." "The classification problem is genuinely hard." "This is not merely a mathematical subtlety." "Modular flow depends on both the state and the algebra." A paragraph often opens with such a sentence of under twelve words, then expands.
2. **Alternation of a short framing sentence with one long precise sentence** carrying the mathematics inline. The long sentence never carries more than one idea; the short one tells the reader what the long one is for.
3. **Explicit pointers to what matters.** "The key lesson is...", "The essential point is to keep track of...", "The key subtlety is that...", "The central question is:", "The point is that...", "Two features ... are worth emphasizing. First, ... Second, ...", "We highlight two structural features of the analysis." These pointers are used about once per page and are the reader's handholds.
4. **Every definition is followed by its meaning.** A formula never stands alone. After E_AB = S_AB/(S_A × S_B): "Non-trivial elements of E_AB represent transformations on A that can only be undone by a compensating transformation on B." After a stabilizer correlation: "In this way, a stabilizer implies that individual measurements performed on different parts of the system are perfectly correlated." The paper must do the same for every instrument: definition, then what a value of it means.
5. **A concrete example immediately after an abstraction.** "As a concrete example, consider the Bell state..." "To fix ideas, we begin by defining ... for bipartite systems, and illustrate with the simple example of a two-qubit system." "Let us illustrate this with an example." "As a simple check, note that when the perturbation is localized entirely in the future wedge ... the horizon contribution is zero. This is consistent with the results of Chapter 3." The check is stated AND its consistency with an earlier result is named.
6. **Rhetorical questions as hinges, answered at once.** "What physical properties does an entanglement group capture?" "What group does one get?" "How does this come about?" "One might inquire about the origin of the difference. Shouldn't a (fully) separable k-party reduced density matrix imply that the k-party entanglement group is trivial? As we will show in section 2.4, this is not quite the case." The question is the reader's question, asked for them, and the answer follows in the same paragraph.
7. **Anticipated objections are voiced and resolved in place.** "One might worry that (4.21) and (4.22) are inconsistent with the invariance of the state under modular flow. ... This means there is a cancellation, and the expectation values ... also vanish, as required." "This claim might appear surprising, since the change of variables involves the complex parameter w. The point is that..."
8. **Graded, specific hedges.** "It is widely expected that", "We expect this type of relation to generalize", "So it seems plausible that (3.17) is correct in general. We will proceed assuming this is the case.", "It is tempting to conjecture", "It is tempting to speculate", "remains to be seen", "does not seem to be necessary", "one possible resolution is". Each hedge says exactly how much is known; a hedge is never a vague "may" attached to a strong claim.
9. **Honest scoping in plain words, at the point where it applies.** "For simplicity we will restrict to the case where Δ and n are integers." "Beyond this, we will not be too specific about requirements on f." "As a warning, at non-generic spectator positions the correlator can be singular. We leave these singularities aside for now and return to analyze them in the next section." "We defer a complete treatment ... to future work." Assumptions are labeled as assumptions the moment they are made.
10. **Paragraph openers name the move.** "To resolve this singularity, we introduce a boost cutoff Λ and decompose the integration contour." "To extract this behavior, we make a change of variables." "To verify this, note that..." "To translate this into a bound on the transverse scaling of the flow, we write..." The infinitive of purpose is the dissertation's characteristic paragraph opener.
11. **Transitions are explicit and brief.** "We now turn to...", "Going forward, it will be convenient to...", "With this background in place, ...", "Finally, we make contact with the perturbative results obtained in the rest of this chapter.", "We conclude by discussing...", "This completes the verification of...", "To summarize, we have shown that..."
12. **Citations are agents in sentences, credited for a specific result.** "Bekenstein [6] argued that a black hole carries an entropy proportional to the area of its event horizon." "Fredenhagen [45] proved that..." "The relevant formula was derived by Sarosi and Ugajin [41], building on work of Lashkari [44]." Never a bracket dump ("[3–9]") after a vague clause. Related work is explained as a sequence of specific results that lead to the problem.
13. **Physics analogies as explanation, one sentence long.** "This is analogous to how a small correction to an ODE can change the fixed-point structure if the unperturbed vector field vanishes at the fixed point." "in much the same way that the behavior of a many-body system can be controlled by the existence of a nearby critical point." The analogy is precise and immediately dropped; it is never decoration.
14. **Contractions appear sparingly, inside explanation.** "It's straightforward to check", "It's convenient to set", "We'd like to understand", "doesn't", "won't". For the paper: allowed rarely in explanatory prose, never in a claim, a result sentence, an abstract or a caption.
15. **Parenthetical clarifications are short and stay in the sentence.** "(which is a negative definition)", "(up to a sign)", "(there are finitely many strata for a given system)". No footnote-length parentheses; footnotes carry technical asides ("Having a local stabilizer is crucial so the invariance survives partial tracing.").
16. **No em-dashes for drama; colons for consequence.** "The verification of the KMS conditions establishes the uniqueness of the result, by the uniqueness theorem for KMS states." "The horizon contribution provides the constant term that cancels ... restoring the boundary condition ε±(0) = 0." Causes and consequences are joined by "so", "thus", "therefore", "because", "precisely because", "which means".

## 3. Paragraph- and section-level markers

- **Section openings state what the section does and how it is organized**, in one paragraph: "The chapter is organized as follows. In §4.2 we derive the horizon contribution ... In §4.3 we give a physical interpretation ... We close in §4.6 with a discussion of ..." The paper's sections do the same in two or three sentences.
- **Section closings connect forward.** "This is the subject of Chapter 4." "We study the calculation for generic modular flow in detail in §5.2." Every section hands the reader to the next.
- **Definition → properties → statement → check.** A definition is given with its notation, its properties are listed ("This classification scheme has a few properties." followed by three bullets), the main statement is made, and an independent check is performed ("which independently confirms the validity of the results"). The independent check is a signature of the voice: a result is not done until a second route confirms it.
- **Results are summarized in a bold run-in ledger.** The conclusions chapter states each result under a bold lead ("**Classification.** ... **Isomorphism theorem.** ... **Monogamy.** ...") in one to three sentences each. Future directions use the same device ("**SLOCC classification.** ... **Continuous variable systems.** ..."). The paper's results and limitations sections use this pattern.
- **One thesis sentence at the end.** "The central theme of this thesis is that entanglement is characterized by algebraic structures ... In both settings, entanglement manifests as a symmetry of the state." The closing paragraph of the paper states the one idea the reader should keep.
- **Speculation is quarantined and labeled.** Conjectures live in their own subsection ("An all-orders conjecture"), are stated as conjectures, and are followed by the evidence for and against ("Several pieces of evidence support this conjecture. First ... Second ... Third ... A fourth piece of evidence ...", then "Whether they persist beyond first order ... remains to be seen"). Nothing speculative leaks into a results paragraph.

## 4. Mathematical writing markers

- Conventions are fixed once and named as conventions: "and we adopt the convention ... throughout this thesis."
- Every symbol in an equation is defined in the "where" clause that follows it, in the order it appears.
- Intermediate results are numbered and referred back to by number; a derivation says which earlier equation each step uses ("From the CPT transformation (3.3) we have", "using (4.17) and (4.18), one finds").
- A derivation narrates the difficulty before solving it: what the naive step would be, why it fails ("this produces a non-integrable singularity at w = 0 for any n ≥ 1"), and what the fix is.
- Notation is introduced with a sentence of motivation ("To save on writing we'll denote ...", "It's convenient to set ...").
- Regularity assumptions are stated as "the minimal requirements" at the point of use, not buried in an appendix.

## 5. Tone: what the voice never does

- Never hypes, never ranks itself against the field in a sentence ("we outperform", "beats"). Comparisons are stated as measured facts on named protocols.
- Never softens a negative result: the conclusions state what "remains an outstanding challenge", what "remains open", what "does not seem to reduce to a boost".
- Never uses filler ("it is worth noting that", "interestingly", "importantly" as sentence adverbs). When something is important the voice says why in the same sentence ("Importantly, for the general modular flow OPE calculation, we evaluate the flow in the horizon limit rather than the strict entangling surface limit" is the one exception, and it is followed by the reason).
- Never lists without a frame: bullets follow a sentence that says what the list enumerates ("The possibilities are:", "Below we will establish").
- Never lets a number stand without its meaning, and never lets a formula stand without a sentence after it.

## 5a. The honesty directive (the PI, 2026-09-08: "keep the paper as honest as we can, since that's the best way to portray the research integrity to contribute to the frontier")

Honesty is a property of every sentence, not a section. Concretely:

- A number is reported on the set it was measured on, and the set is named in the sentence (the 64-step row is "on a 100,000-puzzle subsample" until a full-test row exists; it is never promoted by rounding the protocol).
- A single-seed number says so until the seed triple lands; a floor is reported with every contrast; a contrast inside twice the floor is called flat, never "a trend".
- Costs travel with gains: the compute column stays in the abstract; the memorization of the corpus stays in the laws; coverage columns are labeled as coverage; the residual-selected column is verification-free only where the text says it reaches the verifier.
- Comparators are read through our evaluator at a named protocol and their own published numbers are quoted beside ours; concurrent work that shares an idea (SE-RRM's symmetric state) is credited in the abstract's neighborhood, not buried in related work.
- Negative and flat results are stated as plainly as positive ones (RI hurts the coverage leg; the anchor rows are flat on cold; the width arm does not help), and a kill that fired is reported as a kill.
- The selection instrument's own artifact (the 5-point seed spread that was the 64-puzzle monitor's tie) is told as what it was, with the corrected instrument named.
- Predictions registered before data are scored, misses included, in the appendix; the analyzer that adjudicated is named and was not edited after the registration.
- Nothing is asserted about a mechanism without the instrument that read it; nothing is asserted about the field without the row that measured it.

## 5b. Program jargon that must not enter the paper (the PI's correction, 2026-09-08: "load-bearing" is not my vocabulary)

The ledger's working vocabulary is not the paper's. Replace on sight: **load-bearing** → part of the result / what the result rests on; **the field** (as a noun for the community) → existing models, the released models, the literature, or the named authors; **suite** → a set of instruments; **arm** → variant, run, model; **battery** → the set of measurements; **lens**, **verdict**, **letter**, **kill** → the measurement, the outcome, the rule's reading; **stall** → a puzzle the model fails / a trajectory that stops changing (define before reuse); **attractor** → fixed point (basin is fine, the dissertation uses it); **cold** → single pass from the puzzle's start (define before reuse); **port / back-port** → carry over, extend; **spurious** → wrong (fixed point); **lever** → mechanism, choice; **funnel** → the multi-start coverage curve; **regime** (for a training recipe) → training recipe; **binding** → what limits; **cadence** → interval; **bank / banked** → saved, recorded; **rung / night / campaign** → run, experiment. The test: would the dissertation's chapter 4 use the word? If not, it needs a definition or a plainer word.

## 6. How this maps onto the paper (the register shift)

The dissertation is hep-th prose at book length; the paper is a machine-learning conference paper with the same voice compressed. Keep everything above and add the following compressions:

1. Each paragraph carries one claim; its first sentence is the claim; the evidence follows; the last sentence says what the claim means for the next step.
2. Numbers enter prose only when they change what the reader concludes; the table carries the rest. A number in prose always carries its protocol (set, depth, draws, weights) in the same sentence or the table it points to.
3. Instruments are introduced the dissertation's way: definition, meaning, a one-line example on a known grid, then the law it reveals. Definitions live in numbered `definition` environments; laws in numbered `law` environments; each law's statement is one sentence and its evidence is a table or a figure.
4. Related work is a narrative of results, as in §1.1 of the dissertation, ending at the exact gap the paper fills.
5. Limitations are the "Future directions" register: the honest ledger of what is open, with what would settle each item.
6. The abstract follows the dissertation's abstract: one paragraph of context, then "In the first part ... In the second part ..." replaced by "We ... We show ... We measure ...", with the concrete objects named (the cell, the instruments, the laws) and at most three numbers, each with its protocol.

## 7. Calibration sentences (write like these)

- "The area law is remarkable because it relates a thermodynamic quantity (entropy) to a geometric quantity (area)." (a strong word, justified at once)
- "These two facts, taken together, suggest that the geometry of spacetime is built from entanglement." (two facts, one inference, the verb "suggest")
- "This idea has been made precise over the past two decades, largely through the AdS/CFT correspondence." (history in one sentence, credit in the next)
- "For the vacuum state, the modular Hamiltonian is known explicitly and generates a geometric flow. For excited states, it is not known in closed form, and new methods are needed." (the gap, stated as two parallel sentences)
- "We propose this as the defining feature of entanglement." (the proposal, six words)
- "Not all stabilizers reflect entanglement." (the objection the reader was about to raise)
- "The classification problem is genuinely hard." (a judgment, plainly)
- "So it seems plausible that (3.17) is correct in general. We will proceed assuming this is the case." (the assumption, owned)
- "This provides the physical intuition behind the horizon contribution (4.12): each term in the sum is a light-ray moment, weighted by a coefficient that counts the order of the Taylor expansion near w = 0." (interpretation after derivation)
- "The chirality of the stress tensor is what enforces geometric flow. This is a property of the operator algebra, not merely of the underlying state." (mechanism named, then its scope)
- "Whether they persist beyond first order, or are suppressed by the exponentiation, remains to be seen." (the open question, exactly sized)
