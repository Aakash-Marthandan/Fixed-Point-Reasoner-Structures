# Registration: solving as first passage — four predictive tests on banked records (2026-09-19 ~15:40Z, before the data)

**Why.** The PI (2026-09-19): "let's do the runs that'll strengthen the spine from both your ideas and the new spine … We'll keep the best ones to form a narrative around them." The merged spine (`paper/documentation/PAPER_SPINE_MERGED_2026-09-19.md`, move 5) claims that what depth and restarts buy in a recursive reasoner can be read from two distributions: the waiting time to the first fully right grid, and the per-start success probability over random restarts. Today two of my narratives were post hoc and were cut back by an audit. So this claim enters the paper only if it PREDICTS held-out data. Goal: a MEASUREMENT for paper 1. $0: banked per-puzzle records only; no model is run.

**Known before this registration (so the tests are fair about what is blind).** Aggregate accuracies at 16 / 64 / 128 / 256 iterations for every model below (Table 1; e.g. the width-192 seed 0: 99.20 % at 64 and 99.60 % at 256 on the 5,000; EqR 93.50 % and 95.88 %), the k = 128 selected and verified accuracies and the one-restart accuracy of the three headline seeds and EqR, the count of solved-then-unsolved transitions on the full set, accuracy by difficulty in aggregate (the corpus's yields). NOT known to me: the shape of any survival curve between those depths; any fit or its extrapolation error; anything by difficulty band; any per-puzzle distribution over restarts; coverage or selected accuracy at k = 2, 4, 16; any correlation of waiting time with the rating; any between-model agreement of waiting times. I have inspected array SHAPES only (the inventory in the session log).

**Data (all banked; identical puzzle ids asserted).** (D1) per-step exactness to 256 iterations on the SAME 5,000 test puzzles (seed 20260822) for nine models: the width-192 seeds C5, C7, C8 (46k); the width-384 seeds C0, C2 and the field-attention model C4; the released EqR, CGAR and TRM (alphaXiv) weights. (D2) 128 random restarts at 64 iterations on those same 5,000 puzzles, with per-restart exactness and residual, for seven of them (not CGAR, TRM). (D3) per-step exactness to 64 iterations on all 422,786 test puzzles for seven models (C2, C4, C7, C8, EqR, CGAR, TRM), with the tdoku rating. Packed bits are little-endian; the tool asserts the last bit equals the record's terminal exactness and checks the record's own first-exact field before reading a curve.

**Definitions.** The waiting time T of a puzzle is the first iteration at which its grid is fully right (all 81 cells), from the fixed start; a puzzle with no such iteration inside the horizon is censored there. (Terminal and ever-solved differ by at most 14 puzzles in 422,786 on these models; both are printed.) Survival S(t) = share of puzzles with T > t.

## The four tests

**T1. Depth scaling from the tail of the waiting time.** Fit on iterations 9–64 only (left-truncated at 8, right-censored at 64), by maximum likelihood on the discrete waiting times, three families fixed here: **Lomax** S(t) = (1 + t/τ)^(−α), which is what a spread of escape rates across puzzles gives (primary); **geometric** S(t) = e^(−κt), one escape rate (the baseline); **Weibull** S(t) = e^(−(t/λ)^k) (secondary). Predict the number of puzzles still unsolved at 128 and at 256 from the count unsolved at 8. No parameter is tuned after the first look; the window (8, 64) and the families are fixed now.
- PASS-a: the Lomax prediction of the unsolved count at 256 is within a factor of 1.5 of the observed count on at least 7 of the 9 models. Credence 0.50.
- PASS-b: at 128, within a factor of 1.3 on at least 7 of 9. Credence 0.50.
- PASS-c: the Lomax prediction at 256 is closer (absolute log error) than the geometric one on all 9. Credence 0.90.
- By rating band (0, 1–9, 10–29, 30–59, 60 and above): printed, descriptive.

**T2. Restart scaling from a few restarts.** From each puzzle's FIRST 16 restarts, fit the distribution of per-start success probability across puzzles: a beta-binomial, and a zero-inflated beta-binomial (a share of puzzles that no start solves). Predict the number of puzzles with no successful start among all 128. Held-out log-likelihood of restarts 17–128 decides between the two.
- PASS-a: the better of the two (by held-out log-likelihood) predicts the uncovered count at 128 within a factor of 2 (or, where the observed count is 0 or 1, within 2 puzzles) on at least 5 of the 7 models. Credence 0.45.
- PASS-b: the zero-inflated model has the higher held-out log-likelihood on at least 5 of 7. Credence 0.60. (If it does, the per-start success probability has a mass at zero: some puzzles are waiting, some are not reachable from this start distribution within 64 iterations.)

**T3. Depth against restarts at equal compute.** On the same 5,000 puzzles: one pass of 64k iterations against k restarts of 64 iterations, for k = 2 and 4, the restarts scored two ways: some restart is right (checked by Sudoku's rules), and the restart with the smallest residual is right (no verifier). First k restarts in their stored order; residual ties to the first index.
- PASS-a: at k = 4 the verified restarts beat the single deep pass on all 7 models. Credence 0.70.
- PASS-b: at k = 4 the residual-selected restarts are at least as good as the single deep pass on the three headline seeds and on EqR. Credence 0.55.
- Whichever way it falls is the result; the paper reports the exchange rate it finds.

**T4. Hardness as an escape rate** (the precedent: \citet{ercsey2012chaos}, where a deterministic Sudoku solver's transient-chaos escape rate measures a puzzle's hardness). On all 422,786 puzzles, seven models.
- PASS-a: the rank correlation between the tdoku rating and the waiting time (censored puzzles ranked last) is at least 0.30 on all 7. Credence 0.60.
- PASS-b: the tail escape rate (geometric fit on 9–64, per rating band) falls strictly from each band to the next harder one on all 7. Credence 0.55.
- PASS-c: waiting times agree between two seeds of one model (C7 against C8) at a rank correlation of at least 0.50, and between ours and EqR at at least 0.30. Credence 0.50 and 0.55. (Is the waiting time the puzzle's, or the model's?)

**Descriptive, no prediction:** the count of solved-then-unsolved transitions to 256 iterations on the 5,000; the survival curves themselves; the fitted parameters; η = −log₁₀ κ per band.

## What the paper says under each outcome (written before the data)

- **T1 passes:** "accuracy at 256 iterations is predicted to within a factor x in the failure count from the first 64 iterations; depth scaling is the tail of a waiting-time distribution with a spread of escape rates". **T1 fails:** the miss is reported, depth scaling is shown descriptively, and the waiting-time family is not claimed.
- **T2 passes:** "128 restarts are predicted from 16"; if the zero-inflated model wins, "a share of puzzles has no successful start, so restarts stop paying there". **Fails:** reported; restart scaling stays descriptive.
- **T3:** the measured exchange rate is reported whichever way it falls; if residual selection loses to depth while verified restarts win, that is the selection gap seen at equal compute.
- **T4 passes:** the transient-chaos precedent is cited as the physics reading of hardness, as a correspondence of observables (theirs is a hand-built continuous-time solver; ours are learned maps). **Fails:** the precedent leaves the framing.
- If T1 and T2 both fail, move 5 of the merged spine shrinks to the selection-gap result and the paper loses nothing else.

## Outcome (2026-09-19 ~16:00Z; `runs/analysis/first_passage_20260919/{report.txt, report.json}`; the tool's selftest 10/10 on synthetic populations with known parameters; five mutants killed before the run: big-endian bits, 0-based waiting time, left truncation ignored, censored puzzles dropped, largest-residual selection; every number below is printed by the tool or by the paired read recorded in the session)

**T1. Depth scaling is NOT predictable from the first 64 iterations by a two-parameter tail.** The Lomax fit went to its boundary (τ → 0) on every model: on iterations 9–64 the survival curve is a pure power law, S(t) ∝ t^(−α), with α = 1.18–1.32 on the three width-192 seeds, 0.93–1.03 at width 384, 0.57 on EqR, 0.41 on CGAR and 0.19 on the released TRM. Extrapolated, it UNDER-predicts the failures at 256 iterations by 2.2–3.7× on our models (C5: predicted 5.6, observed 20) and by 1.1–1.4× on the released ones: past 64 iterations the solve rate slows faster than the early tail says. One escape rate is wrong by orders of magnitude on all nine (it predicts 0 failures at 256 where 18–687 are observed): there is no single escape rate. The Weibull fit did not reach an interior optimum and is not reported. Observed unsolved of 5,000 at 8 / 16 / 64 / 128 / 256: C5 539 / 208 / 41 / 31 / 20; C7 603 / 265 / 57 / 33 / 25; C8 565 / 225 / 39 / 25 / 18; EqR 1,036 / 696 / 325 / 254 / 206. **Solved-then-unsolved transitions to 256 iterations on these 5,000 puzzles: 0 on all nine models.**

**T2. Restart scaling IS predictable from 16 restarts.** Puzzles with no successful start among 16 / 32 / 64 / 128 restarts: C5 10 / 9 / 8 / 6; C7 12 / 10 / 7 / 4; C8 11 / 8 / 8 / 7; C0 26 / 22 / 18 / 12; C2 19 / 13 / 10 / 10; C4 26 / 21 / 20 / 17; EqR 105 / 85 / 66 / 58. The better model (by held-out log-likelihood of restarts 17–128) predicts the count at 128 within the registered tolerance on all seven (7.7, 2.5, 10.4, 9.7, 15.8, 23.4, 47.4). The zero-inflated form wins on 5 of 7 with a zero mass of 0.15–0.46 % of puzzles on ours (0.03 % on EqR). Critique: the counts are small on our models (4–17 puzzles), so a factor of two is a generous tolerance there; EqR (58 observed, 47 predicted) is the informative row. "No success in 128 restarts of 64 iterations" is a finite statement about this start distribution, not unreachability.

**T3. At equal compute depth and restarts are interchangeable on our models; restarts win on EqR.** One pass of 256 iterations against the first four restarts of 64: C5 99.60 against 99.66, C7 99.50 / 99.52, C8 99.64 / 99.66, C0 99.14 / 99.16, C2 99.34 / 99.38, C4 99.32 / 99.34, EqR 95.88 / 96.40. The registered letters PASS, but paired on the same puzzles the differences on our six models are 3–10 puzzles each way (exact McNemar p ≥ 0.23 at k = 2 and k = 4): NOT a resolved advantage. On EqR four restarts solve 61 puzzles the deep pass does not against 35 the other way (p = 0.010). Residual selection costs nothing at k ≤ 4 on any of the seven (it equals or is within one puzzle of the verified score). The puzzles each route solves are partly DIFFERENT puzzles.

**T4. The difficulty rating is NOT the escape rate; the waiting time IS largely the puzzle's.** Rank correlation between the tdoku rating and the waiting time on all 422,786: 0.357–0.371 on ours, 0.337 EqR, 0.315 CGAR, 0.289 released TRM. The tail escape rate barely moves across the four rated bands (width 192: 0.070 → 0.068 → 0.063 → 0.059, monotone on two of our models only; flat or non-monotone on the released ones; the unrated band has too few puzzles at risk to read). The rating acts mostly on whether a puzzle is solved in the first eight iterations. But two seeds of our model agree on waiting times at ρ = 0.540 and OUR model agrees with EqR's released weights at ρ = 0.537: two differently built and trained models rank puzzles by waiting time more like each other than either does like the search solver's rating.

**What this does to the spine, by the rules written before the data.** (i) T1 failed, so the waiting-time FAMILY is not claimed and depth scaling is shown descriptively; what is kept is measured: heavy-tailed waiting times with no single escape rate, returns to depth that shrink past 64 iterations, a solved state with no exit in 256 iterations on 9 × 5,000 trajectories. (ii) T2 passed: "coverage at 128 restarts is predicted from 16" enters, with EqR as the informative row and the small-count caveat on ours. (iii) T3 enters as the measured exchange rate: interchangeable on ours, restarts ahead on EqR, no selection cost at small k. (iv) T4-a and T4-b failed, so by the registered rule the transient-chaos reading of hardness LEAVES the framing; T4-c stays as a finding about puzzles (a shared, model-independent ordering of waiting times that the rating only partly captures).

**Critique of my own registration.** I gave T1-a a credence of 0.50 and it was not close on our models: I assumed the tail seen by iteration 64 continues. It does not, and that is the more useful fact for a practitioner (do not extrapolate a depth curve). T3's letters were written as strict inequalities with no margin, so they "passed" on differences of two puzzles: a registered contrast needs its own floor (the lesson already in the register; I did not apply it). T4-b's band structure put the unrated band first, where almost nothing is at risk after eight iterations; the four rated bands are the fair read and they are reported as such.
