**Why.** Professor Raja asked four questions about the paper's central observation (most cells committed at the first iteration): why it is not obvious, what it says about the solution process, what happens when the first commitment is far off, and why it matters at scale. Answering them from the banked per-iteration records (`runs/analysis/suite_ckpt_dyn_2026090{7,9}.jsonl`, `..._20260910.jsonl`) showed that the mechanism is *commit, then repair*: on puzzles a model goes on to solve, 22–34 % of the empty cells are committed and wrong after iteration 1 and are repaired by iteration ~8; on puzzles it fails, 45–74 % are, and cell accuracy stays flat at 35–45 % through iteration 64. The PI (2026-09-17): "run the basin analysis that you need to get more concrete details on this." The goal is a MEASUREMENT for paper 1 (no accuracy target; nothing is trained); $0, the Mac's CPU, banked checkpoints only.

**What is already known (disclosed; read before this registration).** The group means above (solved vs failed puzzles, by model, 128 puzzles). Unknown and registered below: the shape of the dose–response, the within-puzzle contrast, the variance split, the corrupted-start ladder, and the locality of the constraint signal.

**What.** `tools/lens_repair_radius.py` (selftest on hand-built records, the printed strings asserted). Two models: the width-192 symmetric DEC, seed 0, its selected checkpoint (`runs/pretrainchamp_C5/ckpt_046000.pkl`, EMA) and EqR's released weights in our evaluator (`runs/field_ckpts/ported/eqr/ckpt_latest.pkl`, EMA). Puzzles: the evaluator's 512 rating-stratified test puzzles (seed 20260821). Evaluation exactly as the paper's: no halting, no inference noise, 64 iterations. For a run, E is the set of empty cells, e_t the share of E whose top digit is wrong after iteration t (1-based), committed means top-digit confidence above 0.9 (softmax over the nine digits).

(5) is a property of Sudoku's constraints read on the models' own guesses: it says whether repair has a radius because the local error signal stops discriminating when errors are dense.

## Side panel E (2026-09-19, registered before its data): cutting the cross-field channel at inference

*(This section and its outcome were restored on 2026-09-19 after a file rewind removed them from disk; the text is the original, and the ledger entry of the same date records the registration and the result.)*

**Why.** The PI's side question after reading Simplex's factoring-versus-fidelity tradeoff: the symmetric state is a factored representation whose factors (the nine digit fields) are strongly dependent, and the coupling layer is the only cross-factor channel. Commitment after iteration 1 does NOT rise with the symmetric state (91.5–96.4 % against 94–99 % on the unfactored models), so the tradeoff has to be read where the dependence flows. A model trained without the coupling is a pod run; this is the local, fast version: an INFERENCE-TIME ablation of the trained width-192 seed-0 model (its weights never saw a missing message, so this measures how much the trained computation USES the channel, not what a model trained without it would reach; labeled everywhere).
**What.** `tools/lens_repair_radius.py --panel E`: the coupling matrix `fc` of both blocks scaled by α (α = 0 leaves the sub-layer's RMS norm in place with a zero message), by phase: `all` (every iteration), `first` (iteration 1 only: the 42 coupling applications of the first guess), `after` (every iteration but the first: the repair). Conditions: α 0 × {all, first, after}, α 0.5 × all. 256 of the 512 puzzles (every second one), the fixed start, 64 iterations with the usual early exit; the control is Panel A's fixed-start rows on the same puzzles.
**Predictions (credences).** E1 α 0 everywhere: exact at 64 under 20 %: 0.80; commitment after iteration 1 falls under 80 %: 0.50; first-guess wrong share above 60 %: 0.60. E2 α 0 in the first iteration only: exact at 64 at least 90 % (the repair recovers a first guess made without the channel): 0.60, and the first-guess wrong share rises by at least 10 points over the control: 0.70. E3 α 0 after the first iteration: exact at 64 under 50 % (the repair needs the channel): 0.65. E4 α 0.5 everywhere: exact at 64 between E1 and the control, above 50 %: 0.55.
**What settles what.** E1 against the control is the fidelity the trained model buys through the cross-factor channel. E2 against E3 says where that information is spent: on the first guess or on the repair.

**What it says.** (a) Cutting the cross-factor channel LOWERS commitment (92.9 → 80.8 → 60.7 %) and fidelity together, in a dose-response: a cell is committed when the fields agree on its owner, and that agreement is the channel's message; factoring does not buy early commitment. The cross-factor information is spent on the repair far more than on the guess. Scope: one model, one seed, trained weights that never saw a missing message; a model TRAINED without the coupling (`--no-dec-coupling`) was not run.
