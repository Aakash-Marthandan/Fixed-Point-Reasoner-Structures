## 0. The night's outcome in the instruments' vocabulary

Seven decimating decoders of one recipe class, differing by seed, operator, width, orbit and readout, landed within 2.4 pp of each other at D16 and within 1.7 pp at D64; the registered contrasts all read FLAT against the seed floor except width-down (PARITY) and the commit head's calibration (CALIBRATED-INERT). The instruments say why in five sentences, each unpacked below: (1) the decoder class is set by the loop and every arm inherited it whole, so no arm could move the class's per-step behaviour by more than the seed spread (§1); (2) what distinguishes the arms is where they sit on the **revision axis** — the attention operator decides the most per step and revises the least, the narrow cell decides the least and revises the most — and the finish order at depth is the reverse of the decision order (§2); (3) the memorization clock is set by capacity, not by the orbit, so the 30k budget and the 512-puzzle monitor delivered every w384 arm at its peak while the w192 arm was still climbing (§3); (4) verification-free selection is a property of training that exposes the loss to the model's own recurrent states, which the RI rows and the anchor rows do — the same mechanism the field found under other names (§4); (5) a decimating decoder's confidence is a decision, not a probability; a second readout trained on the carried state is calibrated at zero cost, and the joint-readout lens says how: it knows WHICH PUZZLES are failing (a trajectory-level signal both readouts carry, which only the trained head reports as a probability) and not WHICH CELLS are wrong (neither readout does: AUC .63) — the cell-level gap is the decoder's, the puzzle-level gap was the readout's (§5). Two things the instruments caught that the numbers alone would have hidden: the w192 arm's cold-start excursion (§6) and the ignition (§7).

## 1. The loop sets the class; the arms inherit it whole (why every one-variable contrast read FLAT)

**Measured.** On the strat-128 dynamics at t = 64, the seven selected grids commit 93–96 % of the non-given cells at p > .9 after one outer step on solved puzzles and 92–98 % on the puzzles they will fail; they are confidently wrong on 52–74 % of the cells at step 1 on those failures and still on 49–59 % at step 64; readout entropy .02; monotone solves 46–67 %; 3.7–7.0 flips to a wrong digit per cell over the last 32 steps at the stalls; a syndrome oscillating by 13–30 violations; first-exact median 1 (the w192 arm 2), p90 5–10; at every stall 84–94 % of the cells committed with 53–65 % of them wrong and peeling contradicted 100 % of the time (`corpus_lens_20260909.txt`, the champ rows; the same columns on EqR's, CGAR's and alphaXiv's released weights read 95–98 / 93–97, 58–66 → 54–58, 57–75, 3.6–6.0, 16–27, 88–92 / 60–63 / 100). Across the 50-grid corpus the two-axis boundary is untouched: every native SOFT on both axes, every field-loop / DEC / public grid decimating on the dynamics axis, and the champion arms are the decimating class's most accurate members, not a new class.

**Why this makes the contrasts FLAT (inferred).** The one-variable arms changed the operator (C4), the width (C5), the orbit (C3) and the readout (C6) but not the two-timescale loop with its segment supervision and ACT, which is what commits 95 % of the grid at step 1 and propagates from there; the accuracy of a decimating decoder is the accuracy of its first commitments plus what propagation recovers, and the four variables move those two quantities by less than the seed-to-seed variation of the same recipe (1.29 pp at D16), except where they change how much is left to propagate (§2). The field's own one-variable rows say the same: EMA, the digit orbit and the mixer carry the field cell's accuracy; the curriculum trades cold for the selector; depth is free (`Report_2026-09-08_Frontier_Analysis.md` §1.3).

**What others said.** Efstathiou & Balwani (the attractor-landscape reading of TRM) see hypotheses form early and feature activations settle into approximately periodic patterns; Roye-Azar et al. see most of TRM's ARC accuracy at the first recursion step; Lai et al. (arXiv 2609.04963) see trajectories trapped near "grids with repeated digits". All three are the commitment-at-step-1 row and the confident-oscillation stall of the decimating class read through other instruments; the corpus lens now carries the same numbers on seven more grids of a cell the field has not built.

## 2. The revision axis: the operator decides, the width propagates (why C4 led at D16 and C5 at D64–D256)

**Measured.** Exact puzzles after one outer step on the full test: C4 29.8 %, C6 29.4, C3 28.5, C2 25.4, C0 25.2, C1 20.7, C5 17.1 (EqR 12.1, CGAR 19.9, alphaXiv 29.7); after two steps C4 69.0 vs C0 60.8 vs C5 45.7. The dynamics row places C4 at the decisive end (the lowest confidently-wrong rate at step 1 on failures, 52 %; the highest monotone-solve fraction of the w384 arms, 66.9 %; the least churn, 3.72 flips and oscillation 14) and C5 at the revising end (cells correct after one step 61 % against 68–73 on the w384 arms; monotone 45.7 %; 7.02 flips; oscillation 30; first-exact median 2 and the latest emulated halt, 4.07 steps). The depth dividend is the mirror image: D16 → D64 +2.30 pp on C4, +3.26 on C5, +3.07 to +3.40 on the seed arms; D64 → D128 +0.29 on C4 (the one MISS-BELOW of the depth-gain prediction), +0.32 on C5; and by D256 C5 (99.60) is above C4 (99.32) and every seed arm, having started last. On the full set at D64, C5 − C0 = +0.68 pp with 4,651 puzzles only-C5 against 1,757 only-C0.

**Inferred mechanism.** Set attention over the nine fields gives each cell a content-dependent read of the other symbols' states, so more cells are decided correctly in the first application and less is left for propagation; the mean coupling at half the width decides fewer cells per step and keeps more of the grid in play, and the loop's later steps recover it. Where the extra decision is right the operator wins at shallow depth; where the extra decision is wrong it is a confident commitment the class does not revise (Law 2), and depth cannot recover it. That is why C4's depth curve saturates first and why the narrow cell's lead grows with depth: the narrow cell commits less that is wrong. The rating bands say the same on the full set at D64: C5's lead over C0 is present in every band from rating 1 upward (+0.4 / +1.0 / +1.0 / +1.1 pp on the bands 1–10 / 10–30 / 30–60 / 60+) rather than concentrated in one, and its one soft spot is the smallest-givens bin (17–21 givens, a few hundred puzzles: 97.9 vs 99.3–99.6), where a first step that decides less leaves the hardest erasure loads under-determined.

**The w192 arm across its own training (measured; `suite_ckpt_dyn_20260910.jsonl`, strat-128 at t = 64):** at 10k / 14k / 30k / 40k / 46k / 50k the class texture holds (commitment 85–96 % at step 1, entropy .02–.06, 5–7 flips per cell at the stalls except the 40k grid's 2.8, oscillation 13–30) while the solve rate rises 94.5 → 95.3 → 98.4 → 96.9 → 99.2 → 98.4 and the propagation tail shortens (first-exact p90 18 → 12 → 8.5 → 11 → 7 → 7; monotone solves 25 → 34 → 41 → 31 → 46 → 59 %). What the extension bought on this arm was not a different decoder but a deeper propagation of the same one: the puzzles that took 18 steps at 10k take 7 at 46k. The w384 arms had reached that point by 16–28k and then began to lose it (§3).

**What others said.** EqR (arXiv 2605.21488) is the field's slowest starter and best finisher on the frontier rows (12.1 % exact at step 1, +6.6 pp D16 → D64, +1.65 D64 → D128); the same trade appears inside our recipe class between the operator and the width. SE-RRM (arXiv 2603.02193) chose attention over symbols and reports 93.73 at 16 steps at 2M with digit augmentation; C4 is that operator on our loop with ACT, RI and the anchor rows, and it reads 96.70 at 16 steps at 3M without digit augmentation, so the operator's value on this loop is a D16 value, consistent with SE-RRM's 16-step protocol being where it shows. Sotaku (0.8M parameters, 1,024 iterations, 2.7M training puzzles) and FRM (arXiv 2606.29150, "additional recurrent depth becomes productive" under Fixed-Point Forcing) are two more readings that small maps go deep; our depth law reads it on the released weights and now on a 0.79M cell trained on a thousand puzzles.

## 3. The memorization clock is capacity, not the orbit (why C3 read FLAT and why 30k + the 512 monitor worked)

**Measured.** Every w384 arm peaks on the 512-puzzle train-file monitor between 16k and 28k, holds within 1 pp of its peak for 2–14k steps, and, on the three arms run to 50k, declines from ≈ 30k (C1 96.1 → 84.0, C3 96.5 → 86.9, C6 97.1 → 90.0; finals 13.0 / 10.0 / 5.5 pp under the selected grids on the identical 50k; train exact rising to .55–.75, segment CE falling to .11–.29). The 30k arms end within 0.5 pp of their peaks. The w192 arm's monitor is still at its maximum at 50k (97.7; segment CE .53; train exact .25; final = vsel). Night A's w512 arm ended at CE .008 with its plateau over by ≈ 12k. The online orbit (a fresh position-group element per row per step, the n_aug → ∞ limit) changed neither the peak (−0.3 pp vs C0, inside the floor) nor the clock (plateau end 26k vs 30k; the same shape as C1). The registered onset (segment-CE < .05) never fires by 50k on any arm; the held-out decline precedes the CE collapse by ≥ 20k steps. The corrected selection instrument reproduced Night A's A5 exactly on the same recipe and seed (95.09 vs 95.08 at D16, p .62, +0.3 at D64), and the arms whose monitor tied at their maximum (C2, C5) were resolved to the earlier grid by the registered rule.

**Inferred mechanism.** What the DEC fits after its peak is the 1,000 solutions in a position-group-invariant form: the position orbit's size does not enter because the cell learns the group's action from a thousand samples per puzzle and then fits the base solutions through it; the digit orbit does not enter because the cell is exactly S9-equivariant (Night A's A4). The rate of that fit is set by capacity at fixed data and regime (w512 ≈ 12k < w384 ≈ 28k < w192 > 50k), which is the one lever an equivariant, position-generalizing cell has left besides the training length itself; the monitor's plateau end is the observable, and the loss is not (the CE collapse lags). The orbit-invariance instrument of this pass tests the first clause directly, and confirms it (`champ_orbit_invariance_20260910.txt`; the selected grids on the strat-512 at D16 on the CPU route under the identity, two random position-group elements g1 / g2 and one digit permutation g3; flips = puzzles whose solve status changes, against the floor set by re-running the identity on the TPU route):

**What others said.** The field's own weights sit on the same clock: EqR's released model solves 93.4 % of its training thousand against 86.5 % of the test, and alphaXiv's later 60k grid is 3.85 pp under its 50k grid (`Report_2026-09-08_Frontier_Analysis.md` §1.2.6, §1.2.9); PTRM and TRM select their best test checkpoint, which is the same non-stationarity read on the test set. SE-RRM's authors train with digit augmentation their equivariance makes redundant (gaps file §10); the champion night adds that the position orbit's size is redundant too on this cell.

## 4. The selector law: exposure to the model's own states (why every arm's residual selector was clean, and the k = 128 rows)

**Measured.** On the 5k × k32 scans every arm reads spurious ≤ 0.20 % of its wrong draws below the correct-draw median residual (0–3 of 1,522–3,951), AUC .998–1.000, residual-selected top-1 equal to the free verifier to ≤ 0.1 pp (99.28–99.82); on the k = 128 scans of this pass the same holds on all seven (t1r@128 99.48–99.88 = verified@128 to ≤ 0.06 pp; spurious ≤ 0.2 %; AUC .998–1.000), with the w192 arm at 99.88 = 99.88. Random-init draws solve within 0.25 pp of the cold start on every arm. The RI-free plain recipe of Night A reads 4.9 % at seed 0 and 68.6 % at seed 1 (AUC .375: converged wrong draws more converged than correct ones); the field's released weights read EqR 0.00 %, alphaXiv 2.6 %, CGAR 30.9 % (path-dependent: b1 70 vs cold 92); the field cell with RI alone reads 0.0 % (Night A's A2) and with the anchor rows alone .52 (A1).

**Inferred mechanism, and the field's name for it.** RI training draws the carried state from the distribution the multi-init draws use at inference, and the anchor rows train the map from corrupted-solution states it produces itself; both expose the loss to states the model generates rather than to the one trained start. FRM (arXiv 2606.29150, §3) reaches the same mechanism from the other side and names it: under conventional self-conditioning "recurrence can stabilize and sharpen an incorrect solution" because of "exposure bias between the one-step conditioning states used during training and the recursively generated states encountered during inference", and Fixed-Point Forcing, training on rollout-derived carries, turns the convergence residual from chance-level (AUROC 0.50) into a correctness predictor (AUROC 1.00). Our A7 → A5 contrast is the same transition (AUC .375 → .999) with RI and the anchor rows as the exposure; EqR's authors left it at "shaping the landscape" (gaps file §2), and the selector law states the condition: verification-free selection ⇔ the training exposed the map to its own recurrent states. The one thing the champion night adds beyond Night A is the replication: seven arms, four architectural variants, one seed each, all clean, and at k = 128 as at k = 32. The verifier's worth on this cell is 0.6–1.7 pp at k = 32 and 0.7–2.1 at k = 128 (the residual recovers all of it), against the 50–65 pp it was worth on our wide-funnel natives — Speed is Confidence's "89 % of failures are selection problems" (gaps file §9) is a statement about a cell whose funnel is wide; on the DEC the irreducible set at k = 128 is 0.1–0.5 % and the selection problem is already solved by the residual.

## 5. Two readouts on one decoder (why the commit head is calibrated and inert; the joint-readout lens)

**Measured (the registered rows).** C6's softmax readout is the class's on the same trajectories (95 / 93 % committed at step 1, 62 → 54 % confidently wrong at the stalls; top-5 at stalls right 48–68 % of the time across C0–C5 at confidence 1.000); its trained head commits 33.8 % of the free cells at step 1 on the puzzles it will solve (1.5 % wrong) rising to 99.9 % at step 16 (0.0 % wrong), and on the 878 puzzles it fails commits 5.6 % at step 1 (30 % wrong) and 2.1 % at step 16 (21.6 % wrong); cell reliability ECE .050 over the 20k; the head's mean over the free cells separates solved from unsolved at AUC .995 (min-cell .998), precision 99.97 % / recall 99.93 % at c ≥ .9. The head lifts nothing (+0.53 pp at D16 vs C0, inside the floor; +0.08 at D64, +0.11 on the full set; +0.03 at D128) and does not touch the memorization clock (final 90.05 vs 95.54 on the 50k).

**Measured (the joint-readout lens, `champ_commit_joint_20260910.txt`: both readouts on the same cells of the same 512 strat trajectories at D16).** On the 23 puzzles the map fails (1,296 free cells at step 16): the softmax commits 88.3 % of the cells with 52.2 % of them wrong; the head commits 1.3 % with 5.9 % wrong; every head-committed cell is also softmax-committed. **Cell level:** for "this softmax-committed cell is wrong" the head's 1 − c reads AUC .635 and the softmax's own 1 − conf .618 — neither readout locates the wrong commitments; the head's c on a failing puzzle sits at .47 on average with 88 % of its cells in the [.3, .7) bins, right ones and wrong ones alike. **Puzzle level:** the head's mean c is 0.47 on failures against 0.95 on solved puzzles (the softmax's mean confidence .962 vs 1.000), and both rankings separate the two perfectly on this set (AUC 1.000 for mean c, min c, mean conf and min conf; .995 for the head on the 20k). **Calibration:** reliability on the failing puzzles' free cells — the head's [.3, .5) bin is right 40 % of the time, [.5, .7) 56 %, [.7, .9) 71 %, [.9, .99) 94 % (ECE .013); the softmax's [.99, 1] bin holds 1,005 of the 1,296 cells and is right 50 % of the time (ECE .501). **Trajectory:** on the failing puzzles the softmax commits 93 % at step 1 (63 % wrong) and stays there; the head commits 7 % at step 1 (37 % wrong) and ≤ 1.5 % from step 2 on while the cells-correct fraction rises 36 → 46 %.

**Inferred mechanism (revised by the lens).** The class-intrinsic calibrated-commitment gap has two parts that the lens separates. At the puzzle level the carried state carries a clean signal that the trajectory is off — the halting head reads it at AUC .997–1.000, the softmax's mean confidence reads it at AUC 1.000 on this set, and a head trained with a proper scoring rule on the carried state reports it as a probability (ECE .013) where the softmax reports 1.000 — so that part of the gap was the readout's. At the cell level no readout knows which of the confidently committed cells are wrong (AUC .62–.63 for both): the decoder wrote those commitments into the carried state and propagated from them, and the information that would separate a right commitment from a wrong one at a stall is not in the state; that part of the gap is the decoder's. The head is therefore calibrated by ABSTENTION: on a failing puzzle it lowers c on nearly every free cell to about the puzzle's own base rate of correctness (46 %) rather than on the wrong cells, which is why hardening at τ .9 fires on 1.3 % of the cells it should not trust and on 100 % of the cells it should, and why it changes nothing measurable in accuracy: it cannot repair a failure it cannot localize and it cannot hurt a success. Law 2's sharpened form is therefore: the per-puzzle confidence of a decimating decoder is recoverable by a trained readout at zero cost; the per-cell confidence at a stall is not recoverable from the state by any readout we have, and closing it needs a mechanism that changes what the decoder writes (a revision or a search), not a better readout.

**What others said.** Kim & No (the Confidence Shortcut, arXiv 2605.29123): "predicting locally easy digits before their long-range dependencies resolve" — the softmax rows at our stalls, and the lens says the shortcut is not undone by a second readout because the wrong commitments are indistinguishable in the state. Guided reasoning (arXiv 2605.25230) wants cloud-token entropy for abstention; entropy cannot abstain at .02, a trained head abstains at the puzzle level (mean c .47) and that is the usable object. PTRM's Q head, Speed is Confidence's halt-first selection and FRM's convergence residual ("strongly predictive of correctness, AUROC 1.00" under Fixed-Point Forcing) are all trajectory-level signals; the lens says that is the level at which a decimating decoder's state is informative, which is what a domain without a verifier (ARC) can use: a per-instance abstention, not a per-cell repair. LDT (arXiv 2605.08605) removes unsound collapses by construction; the lens says a learned readout cannot remove them after the fact, only report that some occurred.

## 6. The cold-start excursion (what a trained start can do that random starts cannot)

**Measured.** On the w192 arm the monitor's cold rollout fell from 92.8 to 1.2–17 % between 14k and 26k while the training loss did not move (.55–.64); the 20k screen reads cold 17.0 %, a random-init draw 92.8 %, the verifier 99.8 % at k = 256; the census reads 0 % explosions on every grid; the map recovered by 26k and the registered selection stepped over the window. The excursion grid's dynamics (strat-128, t = 64) read 29.7 % solved from the trained start with failures that FREEZE: cells correct flat at 43–47 % from step 1 to 64, 81–88 % committed, confidently wrong 45 → 45 %, entropy .05–.09, 2.0 flips per cell (the intact grids 4.6–7.0), the corpus's lowest syndrome oscillation (12.5) on a low plateau (12–14 violations), and first-exact 6 / 44 on the puzzles it does solve. On the intact 46k grid the same set reads 99.2 % with the class's churning stalls. The raw-weights monitor at w192 swings 30–60 pp between adjacent 2k grids throughout training (91.0 at 30k, 30.7 at 32k, 76.6 at 34k) while the EMA holds 95.7–97.7, and the EMA carries 7.0 pp at w192 against 2.2–3.1 at w384 (the 50k subsample, paired).

**Inferred.** Init-invariance is a trained property of the map (Law 7); the trained start buffers are one more input the map is trained on, and at w192 the optimizer's iterate moves that start in and out of the basin the map keeps for random inits — the same pathology as the d16 natives' cold-basin wander (H-46: cold accuracy fluctuating while the fixed points hold), here at the narrow width where the same learning rate is a larger relative update. The failures inside the excursion are quiet wrong fixed points rather than churning stalls: the D5 continuum's frozen end (ARC's 92 % wrong-stable limits) appears on Sudoku when the START is outside the basin and the map is intact, so the failure class is set by where the trajectory starts relative to the basin, not by the domain alone. The EMA average is what the headline stands on, and the raw row is the instrument that reads the wander.

**What others said.** Lai et al. (arXiv 2609.04963) measure basin geometry by perturbing initial latents on 2-D slices, find fractal boundaries, and see trajectories trapped near nearly-correct saddles; the excursion is a trained start crossing such a boundary in training time, read on the cold row, with the frozen texture on the wrong side and the churning texture on the right side. Their "solvability appears as a bifurcation in training" is §7. EqR's randomized-init training is the reason the random draws stayed inside (the init-distribution law); FRM's exposure-bias account says why a start the map was not trained from is fragile.

**Reading (measured; the stage names inferred).** Three stages, not one. **Stage 1 (2k): a static partial commitment.** The map commits 36 % of the cells at step 1 at readout entropy .28 — between the soft natives' 3–13 % at .44–.59 and the decimating class's 91–99 % at .01–.04 — decides 37–39 % of the cells right, and then does nothing: zero flips over the last 32 steps, a syndrome that does not move, no puzzle solved. **Stage 2 (4k): decimation switches on, propagation is not yet there.** Commitment at step 1 jumps to 94 / 88 % and the entropy to .02–.05 — the class's values, reached in one 2k interval — while only 24 % of the puzzles are solved; the solved ones propagate (cells right 69 → 100 % from step 1 to 32) but the failures freeze exactly as the excursion grid's do (cells right flat at 48–50 %, 0.18 flips, oscillation 1.5): the decoder commits before it can propagate, and a wrong commitment at this stage is a quiet wrong fixed point. **Stage 3 (6k–8k): propagation, and with it the churn.** The solve rate goes 24 → 77 → 94 % in 4k steps; the failures now oscillate (4.1–5.1 flips per cell, syndrome oscillation 25) and lose cells (42 → 36 % right over the rollout at 6k): the revision machinery that recovers the solved puzzles' remaining cells is the same machinery that churns the wrong ones, and the mature class's confident-oscillation stall is born here. Through all of it the training loss moves slowly (.75 → .49) and train exact stays below .30: the ignition is a transition in the held-out solve rate driven by propagation, invisible in the segment CE, and the raw monitor leads the EMA by 2k steps on the way up (53 vs 25 % at 4k).

**What others said.** Lai et al. (arXiv 2609.04963) report that "solvability appears as a bifurcation in training" and ask whether it is the onset of the trapping structure; the answer on this cell is that the bifurcation has two edges 2k steps apart — decimation (2k → 4k) and propagation (4k → 8k) — and the trapping texture they describe (nearly-correct quiet grids) is the failure mode of the first edge, replaced by confident churn at the second. This closes the "settles it" item of the gaps file (§1, gap 2): their bifurcation is the step at which the decoder begins to commit, and it precedes the step at which it begins to solve.
