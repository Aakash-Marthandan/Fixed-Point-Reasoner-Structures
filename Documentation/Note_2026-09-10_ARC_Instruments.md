## 1. Basin existence and its conditioning (D4: inventory-limited vs search-limited)

**Measured.** Retention of the handed truth on our ARC substrates is 18.8 % (RI) and 27.8 % (plain) of val-hard query pairs (9.7 / 29.0 on dev-30), against 1.00 on the DEC's solved endpoints and .95–1.00 on the Sudoku natives at every rating; the ARC era read 29–34 % on the same family. The conditioning is the ARC-era 31× again, at k = 32 on identical queries: a random restart reaches the truth on 25.9 % (RI) and 52.5 % (plain) of the queries whose truth the map retains, and on 0.0 % / 1.0 % of the queries it does not (dev-30 ×9.3 / ×2.4, n small). The field's ARC model retains its demonstration pairs (95.0 %; 98 % of them exact at step 1) and its test accuracy conditions on that only weakly (33.0 vs 13.5 %, ×2.4).

**Interpretation.** On Sudoku the basin of the solution exists for every instance the map has learned to propagate; the question is whether the cold trajectory reaches it (search-limited). On ARC the basin exists for a minority of task pairs, and where it does not, no restart finds it (inventory-limited); the field's model's reproduction of its own demonstrations is not basin existence for the test transformation but the identity table's memory (§7), which is why its conditioning is weak.

**Justification.** Retention is the same instrument on both domains (hand the map the answer, run it, ask whether it stays); the conditioning row uses the same draws that measure coverage. The ×55 is on 144 paired queries; the ∞ on the RI map is 7 of 27 against 0 of 117.

**Physics.** A verification game has one attractor per instance and a restoring force toward it (the constraints re-read at every step); a generalization task has an inventory of attractors, one per rule the substrate holds, and no force at all on task pairs outside the inventory. Retention is the existence test; the oracle is the reachability test; the two coincide on Sudoku and dissociate on ARC.

## 2. Depth: propagation versus nothing to propagate (D9)

**Measured.** The DEC gains +3.3 pp from D16 to D64 and +0.2–0.7 per further doubling with zero regressions in 422,786 puzzles on five arms; its first-exact tail reaches step 5–10 at p90. On ARC our substrates are flat from step 2 (RI 2.8 → 4.9 by step 4 then constant; plain 6.9 ↔ 7.6) with 1–2 of 144 solves lost along the rollout; the field's TRM reads 29.6 → 31.5 → 30.5 (steps 1 / 2 / 16), solves 116 of its 128 at step 1, and LOSES 8 inputs it had solved at step 1 by step 16 (ever-exact 32.9 % vs final 30.6 %); the ARC era's horizon probe read +0 at t = 96.

**Interpretation.** Depth is propagation, and propagation needs a partially correct state whose remainder is determined by the constraints; an ARC output is decided in one or two applications of the selected rule and later steps can only drift, so depth is at best neutral and sometimes harmful. The 8 regressions are the depth law's negative: a decimating map with no restoring force can leave a correct fixed point.

**Justification.** The same exact-by-step column on both; regressions counted with exact pairing.

**Physics.** On Sudoku the constraint field makes the correct grid a contractive fixed point reached by peeling-like propagation (the syndrome falls monotonically on solved trajectories); on ARC the output is a fixed point of a map with no constraint field, so the flow has nothing to propagate toward and its stability at the correct grid is not protected.

## 3. The selector law: the mechanism ports, the value does not (D3, and H-33's landscape-class law)

**Measured.** On ARC, RI training does to the residual what it did on Sudoku: the residual's AUC for exactness is .85 on the RI substrate against .57 on its plain twin, and the spurious rate (wrong draws below the correct-draw median residual) 8.5 % against 59 % — the same direction and size as A7 → A5 on the DEC (.375 → .999; 68.6 → 0.04 %) and X0 → A2 on the field cell. But RI also condenses the draws: 88 % converge to 4.7 distinct endpoints per query (plain 13.5), the per-draw exact rate equals the cold pass exactly (4.86 = 4.86), and the oracle falls from 15.3 % (plain) to 4.9 % (RI); residual selection returns the cold pass on both (4.86; 7.64), the majority 9.0 % on plain. The field's TRM: residual AUC .66–.68, spurious 29 %, residual-selected 31.3 % against the oracle's 32.5 % over 8 inits.

**Interpretation.** Verification-free selection is a property of training (the residual is informative where training exposed the map to its own recurrent states: RI here, FRM's Fixed-Point Forcing there), and it ports. Its VALUE is the coverage it can select from, and on ARC the same training that cleans the residual removes the coverage: an init-invariant map returns the same wrong endpoint from every start. On Sudoku the DEC has both (oracle 99.5–99.9 % and a clean residual); on ARC our RI map has a clean residual and nothing to select, our plain map has coverage and a useless residual, and the field's model has a little of each.

**Justification.** Identical queries, identical draw budget; the E5 spurious definition verbatim from the Sudoku analyzer; the paired only-RI / only-plain counts (oracle: 0 / 15).

**Physics.** Init-invariance is a basin property: RI widens the basin the map holds around each endpoint until every start falls in, which is exactly what makes the residual trustworthy (converged ⇒ in a basin) and exactly what kills exploration when the basin is the wrong one. On a single-attractor landscape wide basins are the goal; on an inventory landscape they are a trap unless the inventory is right.

## 4. Failure classes: frozen versus churning, one mechanism (D5)

**Measured.** ARC failures on our substrates converge (the clean run converges on 94–97 % of queries; 137 of 144 queries fail), to a wrong grid (100 % of the converged failures), with confidence .975 on the wrong cells against .991–.996 on the right ones, 15–16 % of the committed cells wrong, 1.0 flips per cell over the rollout, and 84–85 % of the cells right; the field's model's unsolved inputs sit at a carried-state residual of .017 (solved .008). Sudoku's DEC failures churn: 4–7 flips per cell over the last 32 steps, a syndrome oscillating by 13–30 violations, 53–65 % of the committed cells wrong, and valid-but-wrong grids at ≤ 2 × 10⁻⁴ of the 422,786 (zero on three of the seven arms; mean 1.2–1.8 violations at a stall, givens 100 % intact); the frozen texture appeared on Sudoku exactly once — on the w192 arm's excursion grid, from a trained start outside the basin (2.0 flips, oscillation 12.5, cells right flat).

**Interpretation.** The mechanism is the same on both: a decimating map commits confidently and the wrong commitments are not distinguishable from the right ones in the state (the cell-level calibrated-commitment gap, which the joint lens showed no readout closes: AUC .63 for the trained head, .62 for the softmax). What differs is what happens next: on Sudoku the constraints keep the wrong state moving (the syndrome is a restoring force that never quite reaches a solution), on ARC nothing does, so the wrong grid is a fixed point. The Sudoku excursion shows the ARC texture arises whenever the flow starts outside a basin with no force to return it — a wrong START on Sudoku, an absent BASIN on ARC.

**Justification.** The same C1/C2 columns; the ARC-era 92 % wrong-stable read again as 100 % converged-wrong among converged failures; the valid-but-wrong count on Sudoku is exact over the full test.

**Physics.** The D5 continuum (wrong-and-stable ↔ stuck-and-churning) is the presence or absence of a restoring force on the output: a verification game has one because the rule is a constraint on the visible state, a generalization task has none because the rule is a hidden transformation; the converged-wrong rate is the continuum's coordinate (ARC .92–1.00, memorized Sudoku maps .03–.16, RI + FPA Sudoku ≤ .001).

## 5. The verifier's worth and the vote (D3 quantified on ARC)

**Measured.** What a free verifier would collect is oracle − cold: on the DEC +0.6–1.7 pp at k = 32 (+0.7–2.1 at k = 128), all of it collected by the residual; on our ARC plain substrate +7.6 pp at k = 32 (RI: +0), of which the majority collects +1.4; on the field's model +1.9 pp over 8 random inits and **+17 pp over ~1000 augmentation views** (any view correct 48.8 % of the 41 inputs vs 31.7 % for the identity view), of which the count vote collects ≈ 5 (pass@2 36.6 % on the 40-task subset; the card's 43.0 % on all 400 is the same vote). The vote separates the inputs it solves from the ones it fails by consistency: solved inputs return a median of 5 distinct answers across the views with 76 % of them correct, failed inputs 444 distinct answers with 0.1 % correct.

**Interpretation.** On ARC the coverage a verifier would exploit exists (7–17 pp) and there is no verifier; the field's substitute is the augmentation vote, which works because wrong views disagree with each other and right ones agree — the same condition under which the unverified majority paid on the DEC's screens (+0 to +2.5 where wrong draws disagreed) and failed on EqR's and CGAR's (below cold, wrong draws agreeing). The productive "restarts" on ARC are augmentation views (a different function on each view) rather than init draws (the same function from a different start, which an init-invariant map maps to the same place).

**Justification.** Oracle, majority and residual-selected computed per query from the same draws on both domains; the vote from the field's own evaluator on its own protocol.

**Physics.** A vote over views is a consistency test on the map's symmetry: if the map's output transforms covariantly with the input's transformation, the views agree and the answer is a fixed point of the symmetry action; a wrong output breaks the covariance and the views scatter. That is a weak verifier built from symmetry, which exists on both domains — it is the Sudoku orbit-invariance instrument (position flips at the numerics floor on the DEC) read as a selector on ARC.

## 6. The halting head, the residual and calibration (D14 / Law 2 on ARC)

**Measured.** The field's ARC halting head reads AUC .917 for exactness (Sudoku's released models .998–.9999, ours .997–1.000), halts on 57 % of inputs with precision 52.5 % and recall 97.7 % (agreement with exactness 72 %); its residual AUC is .66–.68 (Sudoku .999). On our substrates the confidence on wrong cells at the end of a failed run is .975 against .991–.996 on right cells. The ID-zero control drops the halting rate from 57 to 20 % with the identity removed.

**Interpretation.** The halting head is trained on the same "is the current answer correct" label on both domains; on Sudoku that label is a function of the visible state (constraint satisfaction), so the head generalizes to unseen puzzles as a verifier; on ARC it depends on the hidden rule, so the head over-halts on unseen tasks (it says "done" whenever the output looks like a task output). The cell-level calibration gap is the same on both (confidently wrong cells); the puzzle-level abstention the Sudoku commit head achieved (ECE .013) is the object that could port, and it has not been built on ARC.

**Justification.** The same AUC on the same halting logits; the confidence columns from the same trace.

**Physics.** A learned verifier is a readout of the state; it is exact only where correctness is a function of the state the readout sees. On a verification game it is; on a generalization task the missing information is the rule.

## 7. The identity, the prefix and memorization (D7 / D11 / D12)

**Measured.** The field's ARC model loses 26 pp when its puzzle identity is blanked (30.6 → 4.8 % on all 419 inputs; 40.6 → 6.3 / 6.3 / 1.6 % on the 64-input control with the correct / deranged / blank / random-trained identity), and reproduces 95.0 % of its own demonstration pairs (98 % of them at the first step) against 30.6 % on the test inputs. The Sudoku prefix is inert (+.6 / .0 / −.1 pp on the three public models at noise 0) and EqR's train-test gap is +6.9 pp.

**Interpretation.** On ARC the identity table is where the model stores the task (Roye-Azar's "zero accuracy without it" read as 4.8 %), and the 95 % reproduction of the demonstrations is memorization through it, not few-shot inference; the test accuracy that survives (30.6 %) is what the shared weights generalize from a memorized task code plus the input. On Sudoku the one rule is in the weights and the prefix has nothing to carry.

**Justification.** The controls are on identical inputs with only the identity row changed; the demonstration rows are the training pairs run through the same forward.

**Physics.** The identity embedding is a task-conditioned attractor selector: it chooses which basin the flow enters; without it the flow lands in a generic one. This is D1's codebook, read on the field's weights: the inventory is indexed by a learned code rather than inferred from the support pairs.

## 8. Similarities the instruments establish (the section's other half)

1. **Both are decimating decoders.** ARC natives commit 78–79 % of cells at confidence > .9 after one step (rising to 96–98 %), the DEC 93–96 %; both solve at step 1–2 or not at all in the one-shot sense, with Sudoku adding a propagation tail. (On Sudoku the same native family committed 3–13 %: the domain, not only the loop, sets the commitment — the freethink's reading, now a measured row.)
2. **Both are init-invariant when RI-trained**, and the field's ARC model is init-invariant through its identity (all-or-none draws); the excursion on Sudoku is the one measured exception, for the trained start.
3. **The selector law's mechanism holds on both:** RI cleans the residual (AUC .85 vs .57 on ARC; .999 vs .375 on the DEC).
4. **The cell-level calibrated-commitment gap is the same on both:** confidently wrong cells at .975 on ARC failures, at 1.000 on Sudoku stalls.
5. **A vote pays under the same condition on both:** where the wrong candidates disagree (the augmentation vote on ARC; the DEC's majority on Sudoku) and not where they agree (EqR / CGAR; the RI map's condensed draws).
6. **Memorization is the model fitting what it is conditioned on, on both:** the identity table on ARC (+64 pp train-test), the thousand solutions on Sudoku (EqR +6.9; the DEC's post-peak decline).
7. **Symmetry is a free verifier on both:** the position-orbit invariance the DEC shows at the numerics floor is what makes the field's augmentation vote work on ARC.

## 9. What the abstract's ARC clauses now stand on (measured rows for §6)

1. *The answer cannot be verified* — the verifier's worth is +0.6–1.7 pp on the DEC and +50–65 on the Sudoku natives, all collectible; on ARC 7–17 pp of coverage exists and the count vote collects a third of it (§5).
2. *What limits a model is which rules it holds a basin for* — retention 19–28 % of query pairs with a ×55 conditioning on the oracle (§1); the field's model's identity-conditioned inventory (§7).
3. *A failure is a confident wrong fixed point rather than an unfinished propagation* — converged-wrong 100 %, confidence .975, 1.0 flips (§4), against the DEC's churning stalls, with the Sudoku excursion showing the mechanism (a start outside a basin with no restoring force).
4. *Which instruments and mechanisms carry over* — port: retention, the C1/C2/C3 rows, the selector law's mechanism, init-invariance, the vote condition, the orbit-invariance test; weaken: the halting head (.92 vs .999), the residual's value (coverage); do not port: depth (flat, with regressions), the residual selector's headroom, the free verifier; ARC-only: the identity, the output-size head, the rule head (§0's verdict column).
5. *What the generalization task asks of the next model* — coverage first (an inventory that includes the task, from the support pairs rather than an identity), a consistency selector built from symmetry (the vote's mechanism, made cheap), and a per-instance abstention readout (the commit head's puzzle-level object); depth and init draws buy nothing (§2, §3).

## 10. For the next model (the freethink's A-4, sharpened by this pass; nothing registered)
