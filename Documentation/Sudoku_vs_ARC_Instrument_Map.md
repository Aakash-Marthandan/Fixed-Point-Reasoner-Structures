## Part 2 — The Sudoku-vs-ARC differences catalog (paper-1 section material)

The field (HRM → TRM → EqR → FPRM) evaluates Sudoku and ARC as one benchmark family. Our instruments say they are structurally different problems, and every difference below is *measured*, not asserted.

**D1 — Rule inventory vs single rule.** ARC is a many-rule problem: hundreds of rule families, each task requiring few-shot inference of *which* rule applies. Sudoku has one universal rule known in advance; every instance is a completion problem. Consequence: ARC's central capacity question is the **codebook** (how many rule-basins can the substrate hold — N, the packing plane, the vacancy floor); Sudoku's is the **landscape of a single attractor** (how reliably is the one basin found). Instruments N/(N,r̄) exist only on ARC; endpoint statistics are their Sudoku shadow.

**D2 — What "generalization" means.** ARC: transfer to never-trained rule families (the rg gates) — the axis where pricing pays (Law 4, seeded at three widths). Sudoku: harder *instances* of the same rule (the rating axis; 1k training puzzles → 423k test set) — the axis the funnel spectroscopy measures. Law 4 is ARC-scoped because Sudoku has no unseen families to transfer to.

**D3 — Verification.** A completed Sudoku grid self-verifies (uniqueness ⇒ any valid completion is *the* solution): breadth is a clean protocol (draw, check, accept), and the many-attempts metric is exact. ARC has no verifier: candidate answers must be *selected* (vote, snap, stability scoring, pass@2 conventions) — the entire ARC conversion stack (populations, snap-vote, MDL selection) exists to substitute for the missing verifier. This is the single deepest protocol difference and it decides which instruments are native to which domain.

**D4 — What limits performance (the landscape-class law, quantitative).** ARC is **inventory-limited**: basins exist for only a minority of task pairs (retention ~29–34 %), and where they don't exist no amount of iteration, temperature, or attempts helps (t=96 extension: +0 solves; anti-Arrhenius; 31× basin-existence conditioning). Sudoku is **search-limited**: basins exist essentially everywhere (retention ≈ 1.0 at every difficulty), and performance tracks the search the instance requires (extended iteration +16/40 at deep levels; per-draw hit rate geometric in rating; vote@k log-linear). The same two instruments (retention; extended iteration) give opposite verdicts on the two domains — the program's cleanest two-sided result.

**D5 — Failure classes.** ARC failures are **wrong-and-stable**: 92 % of converged limits are confidently wrong attractors; the map actively erases handed truth on the old substrate. Sudoku failures are **near-misses**: stuck states carry a median of ~3 constraint violations with all givens intact — incomplete propagation/search, not capture by a wrong basin. (Formal Sudoku wrong-stable instrument = registered-unbuilt; violations serve meanwhile.)

**D6 — Information geometry.** ARC: the throat is a **task-set constant** (785→459 nats, declining with capacity, UV-led) and the priced profile is IR-fattened (the knee) — the code spends on *rule structure and transport*; pricing forces abstraction (anti-water-filling). Sudoku: the throat is **instance-conditional** (801/987/1528 nats at 50/40/30 givens — harder instances route more information; the ledger becomes a per-instance required-computation meter) and the profile is extreme-UV (~.9 at the finest scale) — a local constraint-propagation geometry. Same ledger, different physics readout — the profile is a property of the task distribution, not the architecture.

**D7 — Per-task adaptation.** ARC requires a TTT layer (few-shot rule inference from supports; the basin-preservation constraint on adaptation; keyhole vs LoRA vs full-fit adjudications). Sudoku has no supports and no TTT at all (the EqR/FPRM convention we share). Consequently the whole TTT instrument family (anisotropy probe, KL/EWC anchors, adaptation-budget laws) is ARC-only.

**D8 — What the price buys, by domain (the sign-flip arc).** ARC: a transfer dividend (Law 4) at a mild cold cost — the task's information is an abstract rule, and pricing forces the abstraction. Sudoku: at small scale the price *killed* the solver (the working grid is the information; pricing starved it — H-43); at larger scale the sign flipped to a **stability dividend** (H-44), which at d64 became load-bearing (the un-priced shallow map is broken; the priced twin is whole), with the newly measured cost that **precision and draw-diversity compete for the fixed budget** (H-47). One mechanism, three regimes, fully scale- and domain-dependent — the program's strongest argument that mechanism verdicts must carry their scale and domain scopes.

**D9 — Depth and breadth mean different things.** On Sudoku, depth = constraint propagation and breadth = search over guesses — EqR's D/B axes are mechanistically *justified* there (and our d64 result shows the deep anchored map becomes the best breadth engine at width). On ARC, depth adds nothing (D4) and "breadth" means candidate diversity for selection — a pretraining/inventory property, not an inference-time axis.

**D10 — Difficulty is measurable on Sudoku, not on ARC.** Sudoku's per-instance solver-derived rating enables the spectroscopy family (hit-rate-vs-rating slopes, per-octile votes). ARC has only coarse family labels — no per-instance difficulty axis exists, which limits how sharply ARC results can be stratified. (A measured ARC difficulty proxy is an open instrument idea.)

## Part 2d — Differences catalog: measured additions from rungs 2/2b (2026-08-29 update; PI directive: keep this catalog current as the section's spine)

**D3 QUANTIFIED (the verifier's worth).** The demo cell put numbers on the deepest protocol difference: verified vote vs unverified majority at k=128 — B2-d64 **68.8 vs 18.8 %**, S5-d16 **70.7 vs 5.1 %**. The free verifier is worth +50–65 pp and unverified majority barely climbs with k. The entire breadth dividend rides verification; ARC's conversion stack is the substitute for exactly this. (Figure data banked: `runs/sxd3demo_*`.)

**D5 ABSOLUTE (wrong-attractor contrast).** valid_wrong_frac = **0.0000 across 5 arms × 422,786 evaluations** at d96 — Sudoku never forms a wrong-valid attractor at any measured scale (uniqueness structurally empties the class); failures are stuck partial propagations (mean 17–30 violations, givens 100 % intact). vs ARC's 92 % wrong-stable converged limits. The contrast is no longer statistical; it is exact.

**D11 — What overtraining costs (NEW row, from the cross-era comparison).** ARC: overtraining erodes *transfer* — the optimization-length tax (H-40, confirmed; β-rescue H-41 falsified; budgets frozen at 40k) eats unseen-family basins while in-distribution keeps sharpening. Sudoku: overtraining destroys *stability* (H-45 contractivity collapse, shallow maps; accelerating with width) and *attempt-diversity* (H-47 late-training funnel condensation on priced arms) — while cold keeps improving wherever the map survives. Mechanism of the difference = D2: Sudoku has no unseen families to erode, so the tax re-expresses in the dynamics. Both domains therefore have task-determined optimal training points, for *different observables* — and rung-2b's D1 (anchored T6 @2.5× the collapse-tested budget, six screens across training, val-selection keeping the peak) is the live test of whether the anchor deletes Sudoku's. Registered rules R2b-1/R2b-2 read it either way.

**D8/D9 extensions (rung 2).** The toll's sign arc gained its fourth regime: at d96 the price became the precondition for *training* the deep map at all (free T12 0/4 state-explosion deaths vs priced 2/2 clean — H-48); and the funnel changed owners a second time (d16 shallow → d64 deep → d96 anchored-shallow): breadth ownership tracks *whichever recipe is dynamically stable at that scale*, sharpening D9's "depth and breadth mean different things" into a scale-indexed statement.

**SCALE-MATCHING GAP (PI, 2026-08-29 — the ARC-resume hook).** The catalog's cross-domain rows currently compare Sudoku at d16–d96 (soon d128, 3.71 M) against ARC frozen at d64 (763 k) — the ARC d96 rung was registered-in-outline (rung-1c verdict: A5-class @40k, knee-β, predictions drafted: rg-96 ≥ 23.5 kill < 21, N 42–50, I < 499, knee L1 ≤ .15, η .21–.22) but never launched when the pod pivoted to Sudoku. PI direction: after the Sudoku ladder closes, run the ARC extension (d96, optionally d128) BEFORE the ICLR paper to scale-match D6 (the two throat behaviors), Law 4's fourth width, H-42's registered d96 test, and the optimization-length tax at width — carrying the Sudoku-era instrument back-port (final-map retention, 2k monitors + val-selection, λ_y, Adam-v Fisher; Part 3 item 3) so both domains are read with the same upgraded suite. Cheap by Sudoku standards (ARC arms ≈ 40k steps on a small corpus; d96 rung ≈ $60–100, d128 ≈ $120–200 — inside the reserve).
