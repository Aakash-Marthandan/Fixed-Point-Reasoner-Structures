## X-1. The organizing observation: two dynamics classes, one wall

Put §0 together and the sportC1 gap has a mechanism, not a number. **Our cell is a soft-decision propagator; the field's cell is a hard-decision commit-and-revise engine.** Both solve rating-0/high-givens puzzles by propagation. When propagation stalls — the belief-propagation stopping set, which for the forum-hardest puzzles at ~24 givens is the normal case — our map keeps its options open (entropy .3–.4), makes no progress after step 2, and churns at a marginal Jacobian (λ ≈ 1) for as long as we iterate (256 steps: +1.6pp). The field's map commits (entropy .02), propagates the committed guesses hard (70 % of cells right after one 42-layer step), revises 78 % of its wrong guesses on the puzzles it solves, and when it fails it churns *confidently*; a fresh random init sends it down a different confident path, which is why re-initialization rescues 96 % of its failures and only 22 % of ours. The RI training that gave our maps their +34pp per-draw dividend also made every random init follow the same soft path, so draws stopped exploring. The wall is therefore not capacity and not the optimizer regime (R0 proved that): it is the absence of a *decimation* mechanism in our dynamics, plus the shallow per-step propagation that makes each commitment propagate slowly.

This reframes the D-catalog: on Sudoku the hard puzzles REQUIRE search (rating = backtracks), and search in an iterative map is decimation + revision. Our verified-attempts column was the program's search, externalized (random inits × free verifier); the field internalized a crude version in the dynamics; PTRM re-externalizes it as noisy parallel trajectories with a learned selector and reports 98.75 %.

## X-2. Five lenses on the same object

**Error-correcting codes / decoding (the sharpest lens this round).** A Sudoku puzzle is a codeword of the constraint code with 81 − givens erasures; solving is erasure decoding on a loopy factor graph. Reachability vs givens (§0.1) is our decoder's *erasure-threshold curve*: it crosses 50 % near 26 givens; the field's decoder has no threshold in the tested range (17–35 givens). Our stuck states are BP stopping sets (high entropy, no progress, marginal λ) — the exact failure class of soft-decision iterative decoding; the field's dynamics is closer to a decimating decoder (hard decisions, propagation, revision). Memorization (§0.7) shrinks the threshold from ~26 to ~28 givens — the memorized map is a lookup table for the easy erasure loads, which is what "excess free flux is memorization" (S2) looks like in decoding terms. Consequence: the champion needs a decimation step. The cheapest interpretable form is *verified decimation at inference* (fix the most confident cells, propagate, backtrack on contradiction — ground 7); the training-time form is a hard-decision feedback row (commit the argmax for a fraction of steps, learn to revise) inside the SOT carry.

**RG.** Our per-step map is one coarse-grain/refine pass over two scales (~5–6 layer-equivalents at d128); the field's outer step is 42 applications of a shared operator on a flat 97-token lattice. In RG language they run many RG *iterations* per readout; we run one iteration per readout across a scale *hierarchy*. The measurement says RG time per readout is what CSP propagation needs (70 % of cells correct after one step vs 49 %), and that it cannot be added at inference (K=2 latent cycles without retraining: 1.6 %) — the flow must be trained at the depth it will be run. Inner cycles (K latent passes per readout, trained) are the RG-time graft; the RG *hierarchy* stays (it is what makes the difficulty curve flat and the 3-adic pooling exact). Prediction: at trained K=3, first_exact medians halve and the 21–25-givens reachability rises first (the bin where propagation depth binds, §0.8).

**Holography.** The program's holographic statement is architectural: one seam operator serves every scale (70 % of parameters on the cuts). The field's cell is the temporal version: one operator applied 42 times per step (weight-tied blocks, 2 layers). Their "bulk" is depth in *time*; ours is depth in *scale*. The dictionary suggests the two are complementary, not exclusive: a radial hierarchy with K applications per scale per readout is a bulk with both directions. Nothing here is measured beyond §0.3/§0.9; it is the frame that makes the inner-cycles graft the natural one rather than a copied trick.

**Quantum geometry.** (i) Measurement: I entered this pass expecting our map to *collapse prematurely* (a projective readout each step destroying the latent's superposition). The data kill that reading: our readout is a weak measurement (entropy .5, decaying only on solved puzzles) and the field's is the projective one (entropy .02 at step 1) — and the projective map wins on CSP because Sudoku's information is a discrete assignment: committing IS computing. The soft map's virtue shows elsewhere: no spurious attractors, exact contractive fixed points, a residual selector that works without noise training. (ii) Fisher geometry: weight decay spreads the curvature mass across 50× more directions (PR/n 7e-6 → 4e-4) and prevents memorization; the memorized native map has 0.7–3 % of parameters carrying 90 % of curvature — memorization is curvature collapse onto a low-dimensional lookup. The field cell sits at 1e-2. This is the regularization-side S2 instance with a geometric readout, and it argues for wd 1.0 in the champion regime as a geometry, not a hyperparameter.

**Information theory.** The throat/flux ledger reads the regularized code: R0 routes 30k free nats vs 104–223k on the memorizing twins (5×) with an uncommitted codebook (rule_H .27) and never memorizes. The MDL reading of the whole round: at 1k puzzles the corpus carries ~1k solutions of information however it is augmented; a 3M-parameter map at wd 1e-4 stores them (memorization = the code exceeding the message); wd 1.0 forces the code to stay below the message length and the map to generalize. The field's aug-1000 + wd-1.0 convention is a rate constraint, not a data constraint — which is why our H-49 remedy set (more augmentation, a floor phase) failed and theirs works.
