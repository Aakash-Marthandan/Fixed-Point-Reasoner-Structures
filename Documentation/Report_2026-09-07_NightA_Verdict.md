## 2. The questions, answered in plain words

### 2.1 Does exact symmetry replace the digit orbit on the field's loop? (A3 vs X1, X0; A4 vs A3) — Yes, and it reaches the field's accuracy without it

What we did: the TRM block and its two-timescale loop, unchanged, on a state with nine digit fields per cell, every weight shared over the fields (exact S9 by construction), a mean field coupling per cell, role embeddings for the givens, no prefix, in the field's regime, with NO digit augmentation (A3); the same with digit augmentation added (A4).

What happened: A3 reads **90.04 % at D16 and 94.66 % at D64** on the full test at its selected grid (2.78M parameters). Against the field cell WITHOUT the orbit (X1, 21.20 / 24.23) that is +68.8 pp paired on the identical 422,786 puzzles (only-A3 294,556 / only-X1 3,508); against the field cell WITH the orbit (X0, 86.03 / 92.81) it is +4.0 pp at D16 (37,304 / 20,320) and +1.9 pp at D64 on the 100k subsample. Adding digit augmentation to the equivariant cell (A4, 93.22 / 97.63) moves the selected-grid number by +3.2 pp, inside the seed pair's spread and, per §2.6, inside the corrected floor: an exactly equivariant map cannot learn anything from a relabeled copy, and the measurement agrees.

Why it matters: the digit orbit is worth 65 points to the field's cell (sportC2 X1) because a non-equivariant cell memorizes the 1,000 solutions without it. The symmetric state gets the orbit for free and, on this loop, does better than the augmented field cell at 55 % of its parameters. Per-octile the DEC's curve is flat and above X0's on every octile (A3 85–90 vs X0 79–85 at D16; A5/A7 97.3–97.7 vs X0 89.6–91.9 on rating ≥ 10 at D64).

Letter: EXACT-S9-REPLACES-AUG +PARITY-AT-D16 +PARITY. The compute caveat is §3.

### 2.2 Does the anchor row lift the field cell's selector? (A1 = X0 + FPA k1) — No; it damages it

What we did: a quarter of each batch re-run for one segment from the embedded 20 %-corrupted solution, supervised toward the solution (the anchor CE fell to .005 by 50k).

What happened: cold16 +0.4 pp over X0 at matched seed (inside the floor), verified@128 99.87 — but the residual selector collapsed: 52 % of wrong draws sit below the correct-draw median residual (X0 7.8 %), t1r@128 38.2 (X0 92.5), b1 53.3 (X0 92.1). Its screen tells the same story (spurious 64 %, t1r@256 17.8).

Why (labeled inference): the anchor teaches the loop to be stationary near corrupted-solution states; from a random init the map now settles quickly into whatever it lands on, and a wrong endpoint looks as converged as a right one. The mechanism that gave our native cell its ≤ .1 % converged-wrong draws (H-45's exact contractive fixed points on the answer register) does not transfer to a loop whose selector reads a latent residual. A second reading is in A0: the RI-free field cell's selector is seed-fragile by itself (§2.3), so a k = 4 anchor is not the next arm — RI is.

Letter: LIFT-FLAT. The 55 % credence on SELECTOR-LIFT failed in the opposite direction; the k = 4 follow-up named in L1 is dropped in favour of the RI reading.

### 2.3 What does EqR's RI do on the field loop, one variable? (A2 = X0 + RI σ1) — It buys the selector and init-invariance, and pays 1.4 points of coverage

What happened: cold16 85.65 (−0.4 pp vs X0 at matched seed, inside the floor), D64 92.13; b1 91.99 ≈ cold (the map is init-invariant), spurious 0.0 %, residual AUC .999, t1r@128 98.30 = verified@128 98.32 — verification-free selection at EqR's column — but the verified funnel narrowed from 99.70 to 98.32 (only-X0 detected on the coverage leg), which is the letter's trigger.

This is the D3/D9 law on the field loop with one variable: verification-free selection requires RI training; a verifier does not.

Letter: RI-HURTS by the coverage leg; the reading is RI-TRADES-COVERAGE-FOR-SELECTION.

### 2.5 The DEC memorizes the thousand-puzzle corpus at the field's rate — the trajectory law

Every DEC arm's segment CE falls from ≈ .65 at 5k to .01–.16 at 50k (A3 .075, A4 .124, A5 .162, A7 .070, A8 .008), train exact rises to .70–.96, the halting head halts 64–88 % of rows after 1.2–2.3 segments (the field cell: CE .58–.60, train exact .19–.23, halt 17–25 %, 5 segments), and the monitor peaks early — A4 at 10k, A8 at 12k, A7 at 22k, A5 at 28k — then declines. The finals are 86.2 (A3), 87.2 (A4), 84.5 (A5), 80.7 (A7), 72.4 (A8) at D16 on the 50k subsample (vsel − final +3.8 to +21.4 pp, paired). The decline erases the low-givens capability first: A7 at 21–23 givens 86.7 → 49.5 %, A8 83.9 → 39.2, while 29+ givens stay ≥ 99 — the shrinking decoding radius the sportC2 lens found on our memorizing natives. Width memorizes fastest: A8 (w512) is −1.45 pp below A7 at matched seed at D16, −1.04 at D64, with the deepest final drop and end CE .008.

Reading: an exactly equivariant cell sees the same effective corpus as the field cell without the orbit — 1,000 solutions under the position group — and the field's regime (wd 1.0, EMA, ACT) does not prevent memorization on it at 38 epochs; it delays it to the 10–40k window where the peaks sit. Val-selection is load-bearing on this cell, the EMA row is worth +0.9 to +8.5 pp depending on the grid, and the paper reports the vsel AND final numbers with the curve. Position augmentation is real data for the DEC (it is not position-equivariant), which makes a larger position orbit the cheapest anti-memorization lever for Night B (§7).

### 2.6 The 5-point DEC seed spread is a selection artifact of the 64-puzzle monitor

A3's monitor (`val_t16_ema`, 64 train-file puzzles) tied at its maximum 96.9 at 16k, 20k, 26k and 42k; the chain's later-tie rule selected 42k, after the monitor had dipped to 90.6 at 40k and the segment CE had begun collapsing (.17 at 40k). A7's monitor had a unique maximum (100.0) at 22k. Re-evaluated on the identical strat-512 set at D16 (EMA): A3's 16k grid 93.95 vs its selected 42k grid 89.45 (only-16k 36 / only-42k 13, p 1.4e-3) and vs A7's selected grid 94.34 (11 / 13, p .84). The two seeds agree at A3's earlier tie; the 5.08 pp SPREAD-DEC at the selected grids is the selection instrument's resolution (1/64 = 1.6 pp, four-way ties, the tie-break on the memorization side), not seed noise. The registered rules read the selected grids as they stand; the consequence for reading is that FLOOR_DEC = .0508 was inflated — the corrected floor sits near the .005 clamp, and at their best grids A4 − A3, A5 − A3 and A8 − A7 all lie within a few pp. Registration hygiene for Night B: a 512-puzzle train-file monitor, an earliest-tie rule, the raw monitor as the second key.

### 2.7 Width (A8 = DEC-w512 seed 1; A6 absent)

R-A-6 is NO-DATA by the letter. Descriptively, at matched seed the w512 arm reads below the w384 arm (93.67 vs 95.12 at D16; 97.00 vs 98.04 at D64, both paired, p < 1e-97) and memorizes fastest (end CE .008, final 72.4).
