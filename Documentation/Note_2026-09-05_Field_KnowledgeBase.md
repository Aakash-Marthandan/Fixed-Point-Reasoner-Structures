## 3. Port verification (I12): our JAX `trm_cell` on the alphaXiv weights vs their PyTorch fp32 — 64 strat puzzles, D16
Step-1 max |Δlogit| 0.0017 on logits of scale 19.5 (mean 2e-5); then ×10 per outer step (0.015, 0.21, 0.75, 15, 29, 40 …) with exact-solve agreement 100 % through step 9 and 95.3 % at step 16. The control — PyTorch fp64 vs fp32 on the same weights — shows the SAME growth (0.006, 0.02, 0.20, 1.2, 16, 22, 35 …; exact agreement 100 % through step 12). Reading: the port is exact to fp32 round-off; the field's recurrence is Lyapunov-unstable on churning (unsolved) puzzles — round-off is amplified ≈ 10× per outer step (42 block applications), which is the quantitative form of lens G3's "unsolved endpoints keep moving" and of PTRM's "oscillates in a bounded region". Solved endpoints are unaffected (exact agreement stays high because solved trajectories are readout-stable).

## 4. The 2026 literature the paper must place (each with the mechanism in our terms)
- **SE-RRM — Symbol-Equivariant Recurrent Reasoning Models** (Freinschlag, Bertram, Kobler, Mayr, Klambauer; JKU Linz; arXiv 2603.02193, 2026-03-02; code github.com/ml-jku/SE-RRM, no checkpoints): the state is a (B, K symbols, I positions, D) tensor with ONE shared embedding for all non-special symbols (2 special tokens); each block = attention over positions within a symbol → attention over SYMBOLS within a position → SwiGLU (post-norm); readout = a shared linear to one logit per (position, symbol); 2M params; TRM-style loop H3/L6, 16 supervision steps, random 5 % early stop instead of Q-halting; Sudoku-Extreme 1k × 1000 (their builder inherits HRM's digit permutation ⚠ — so their Sudoku model WAS trained with digit augmentation, redundant under equivariance = our A4 control), batch 272, 10k epochs, lr 5e-4, wd 1: **FSR 93.73 % / cell accuracy 97.58 %** (their TRM row 71.94, HRM 63.53); generalizes 9×9 → 4×4 (95.5 %), 16×16 (cell acc 52 %), 25×25 (31.5 %) — a size-generalization our fixed 81-cell mixer cannot attempt; ARC-1 45.3 pass@2 with 8 dihedral augmentations vs 1000. **Consequences:** (i) exact symbol equivariance on Sudoku is published (March 2026) — the DEC's novelty is NOT the symmetry; it is the decomposition (symmetry × equilibrium objectives × commit head × instruments) and the no-augmentation-at-all row (A3) SE-RRM did not run; (ii) their cross-symbol operator is ATTENTION over the K symbols per cell; ours is the DeepSets MEAN of the other eight fields — the exact confound named in `Note_2026-09-05_NightA_PreVerdict_Labels.md` L3; their 93.7 at 2M in 10k epochs is the external evidence that the symmetric state reaches parity-class when the cross-symbol operator is strong → Night B's set-attention coupling arm is literature-backed; (iii) their size generalization is a new instrument axis for paper 2 (equivariance to the board size).
- **PTRM — Probabilistic TRM** (Sghaier, Parviz, Jolicoeur-Martineau; arXiv 2605.19943): Gaussian noise σ = .3 added to the latent-state input at each supervision step, K = 100 parallel rollouts, D = 64, selection by the existing Q head (best-Q@100 = 98.75; pass@100 = 99.06; deterministic 87.28); failure taxonomy = quick success / delayed success (oscillates then escapes) / failure (oscillates in a bounded region); no code release stated. In our terms: verified-attempts protocol with a learned selector; the bounded oscillation = our churn class (E2), their "escape" = the re-init rescue (E4).
- **CMM — Contraction Mapping Model** (Es'kin & Smorkalov; arXiv 2603.22871, 2026-03-24; no code): TRM-MLP cell reformulated as NODE/NSDE with an equilibrium loss (λ 1), a Routh–Hurwitz trace penalty (λ 1e4 stable z / 10 unstable x), hyperspherical repulsion (λ 1e3), noise σ .01, StableMax, λ_LM 1, λ_BCE .5; B = 250, N_grad 64, N_accum 16, lr 2e-4, 16 supervision steps, AMP: 93.7 % at 5M (CMM512NSDE), 85.4 % at 0.26M (CMM128NSDE, weight-shared); their TRM reproduction 79.1. In our terms: the field converging on our FPA/contractivity objectives; parameter parity is no longer the efficiency frontier.
- **GRAM — Generative Recursive Reasoning** (Baek, Jo, Kim et al.; arXiv 2605.19376; ICLR 2026 workshop; site ahn-ml.github.io/gram-website): stochastic latent trajectories trained by amortized variational inference; **97.0 % on Sudoku-Extreme at 10M with N = 20 sampled trajectories + majority vote at 16 iterations**; their TRM row 87.4 → **90.5 at 320 iterations** (a depth-scaled TRM number the table can cite); N-Queens 99.7 %, graph coloring. In our terms: unverified self-consistency over stochastic draws (the aug-HRM column class), which LLMonkeys/D3 say plateaus without a verifier — GRAM's majority vote works because its draws are semantically equivalent trajectories, not orbit views.
- **Guided reasoning** (arXiv 2605.25230): stochastic perturbation of TRM's trajectories + online reweighting by the early-stopping head: 85.9 → 98.0 without retraining; three label-free diagnostics (local stability, guide alignment, cloud-token entropy). In our terms: the halting head as a selector (our I8 row) and local stability = our Jacobian/residual census.
- **CGAR** (Qasim et al.; arXiv 2511.08653, JAIR 2025): progressive depth curriculum (H1/L2 → H2/L4 → H3/L6 over training) + supervision-weight decay .7^(t−1): 1.71× faster training at 86.02 vs 86.65 baseline (A100). Code + weights released (the instrumented file). The README's "80/20 split" sentence ⚠ conflicts with the 423,168-puzzle test claim; the HF config shows the standard 1k-aug-1000 build.
- **TRM + Mamba-2 hybrid** (arXiv 2602.12078; ICLR 2026 workshop): ARC-1 pass@2 45.9 vs 43.9 at 6.83M; no Sudoku number.
- **Ren & Liu** (arXiv 2601.10679; in the ledger): HRM 55.0 → 96.9 via orbit votes (+18.2), checkpoint bootstrapping (+9.2), augmentation (+4.9); fixed-point violation; conflict energy = our violations instrument.
- Classical decoding references for the lens (⚠ verify at writing): Moon & Gunther 2006 (BP on Sudoku, IEEE Mountain Workshop); Moon, Gunther & Kupin, "Sinkhorn solves Sudoku", IEEE T-IT 55(4) 1741–1746 (2009); Goldberger 2007, "Solving Sudoku using combined message passing algorithms" (Bar-Ilan); Montanari, Ricci-Tersenghi & Semerjian, "Solving constraint satisfaction problems through belief propagation-guided decimation" (arXiv 0709.1667, 2007); Di, Proietti, Telatar, Richardson & Urbanke 2002 (stopping sets on the erasure channel); Palm, Paquet & Winther 2018 (RRN, 96.6 % on 17-given puzzles, arXiv 1711.08028).
