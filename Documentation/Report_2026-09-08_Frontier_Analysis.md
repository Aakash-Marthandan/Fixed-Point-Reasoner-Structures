### 1.2 The design choices, measured on their weights

1. **EMA .999 is the largest single choice on EqR's released model.** The raw Langevin-trained weights read 69.69 at D16 against the EMA shadow's 86.52 (+16.8 pp, paired on 422,786; P18 predicted 1–8). On the field cell reproduced on our stack EMA was +5.2, on the DEC +0.9 to +8.5 by grid. The per-step noise that gives EqR its clean attractors also leaves its raw iterate far from the average; the EMA is doing part of the "equilibrium" work.
2. **The Langevin noise is a training device, not an inference one.** Training noise .01 at eval: −0.40 pp (P16 hit). Their released eval yaml's noise .5: −10.09 at D16 and −2.72 at D64 on the 20k (P16 hit); the same noise raises breadth (the 5k × k32 scan with noise .5 reaches verified 99.4 vs 98.4 without it, k90 93.6 vs 75.1 draws). The paper's B = 1 column is the noise-0 number; the released protocol is a breadth setting misread as a headline.
3. **The reset distribution does not matter once RI/NI training has cleaned the attractors.** EqR's own truncated-normal reset and our Gaussian draw give the same b1 (93.00 vs 93.05), the same spurious rate (0.00 %), the same t1r/verified (0.999 / 1.000) (P17 hit).
4. **The halting head is a verifier; halting on it is a small loss.** AUC(q_halt, exact) at step 16: .9990 / .9991 / .9999 / .9984 (alphaXiv / CGAR / EqR / 60k) with precision 99.9–100 and recall 100 (P14 hit). Emulating ACT halting (stop at the first step with q_halt > q_continue, mean 4.8–5.7 steps) reads −2.24 / −1.72 / −0.78 / −2.27 pp against running all 16 steps (P15 split): the head is right about correctness and slightly early about stopping. The field's own convention, all D steps at eval with halting used only in training, is the better protocol, and the head's real value is as the selector PTRM uses.
5. **Depth is the cheapest accuracy the field leaves on the table.** Per-step exact bits from one rollout: alphaXiv 79.32 → 81.60 → 83.45 → 84.97 → 86.26 at D16 / 32 / 64 / 128 / 256; CGAR 86.10 → 89.42 → 91.71 → 93.17 → 94.10; EqR 86.52 → 90.60 → 93.15 → 95.02 → 95.88. Regressions (solved at some step, unsolved at the last) 0 to 1 puzzle in 422,786 (P7 hit). The D128 and D256 gains are LARGER than predicted (P6 missed above on five of six clauses): a decimating decoder keeps propagating past its headline depth at about +1 pp per doubling, and the field's D = 16 headline undersells its own models by 6–9 points.
6. **"Best test checkpoint" hides the trajectory law.** The released 60k-epoch alphaXiv grid is −3.85 pp under the 50k-epoch grid (75.47 vs 79.32, paired, P4 missed): the field's selection also sits on a non-stationary trajectory, and it selects on the test set. Our val-selected convention with the corrected monitor is the honest version of the same fact.
7. **The curriculum trades the selector for cold accuracy.** CGAR's curriculum (H1/L2 → H3/L6, supervision decay .7) is +6.8 pp over alphaXiv at D16 (86.10 vs 79.32, paired) and CGAR is statistically indistinguishable from our reproduction X0 at D16 (86.10 vs 86.03, McNemar p .18, Jaccard .852). But its map is path-dependent: a random-init draw solves 70.1 vs its cold 91.6 (−21.6 pp, P11 hit), its init radius falls to .80 of cold at ε = 3 (EqR .99, alphaXiv .94; P20), and its residual selector is broken (spurious 30.9 %, AUC .69, t1r falling with k from 75.7 at k8 to 71.3 at k128 while the verifier reaches 99.3) (P10 missed above, P13 hit). The Mac's small gain at ε = .3 did not replicate on the pod route (−1.76). A curriculum that specializes the trajectory to the trained start is the wrong recipe for a model that will be used with random restarts.
8. **The prefix is inert on all three at noise 0** (+0.57 / +0.02 / −0.10 pp on the 20k; P21 missed on EqR only because the Mac's −5.3 was measured under noise .5: the prefix is load-bearing for EqR only when its Langevin noise is on).
9. **The field regime delays memorization; it does not prevent it.** EqR solves 93.40 % of its own 1,000 training puzzles at D16 against 86.52 on the test (+6.9 pp; P19 missed above; the Mac read +4.4 at noise .5 and called it "no memorization"). With wd 1.0, EMA, ACT and 9!-fold digit augmentation all on, the released model still sits above its test rate on its corpus. H-49's law extends to the field's own weights.
10. **The selector law reads on the three public models exactly as on ours.** Spurious rate per wrong draw at k128: EqR 0.00 % (AUC .999, t1r 98.85 = verified 98.89), alphaXiv 2.60 % (AUC .970, t1r/verified .962), CGAR 30.9 %; HRM on the Mac 12–15 % (P10 hit on two of three). Verification-free selection works where RI/NI training cleaned the attractors; the free verifier reaches 97.5–99.3 on all three regardless (P12: EqR's 98.89 missed the ≥ 99 bar by .11). The unverified majority at k256 reads BELOW cold on EqR and CGAR (88.1 vs 89.5; 84.4 vs 87.7; P23 missed below): the wrong draws agree with each other.
11. **Calibration at stalls is the field's gap too.** The five most confident cells at a stall are right 44.9 / 50.5 / 53.9 % of the time (alphaXiv / CGAR / EqR) at confidence 1.000, entropy .007–.016 at step 1 (P22 split on alphaXiv's .449 below the .45 bar). Every stall of every field model contradicts peeling from its committed cells (100 %).
12. **Class.** On the FULL D64 records the alphaXiv TRM keeps an erasure threshold (g50 18.0 in the tested range, yield 80.7 → MIXED); CGAR and EqR have none (10.7 / 9.4, yields 90.3 / 92.0 → DECIMATING) (P8 hit). First-exact medians on the natural distribution are 2–3 steps, not 1 (P9 missed): the "one-shot" reading came from the rating-stratified sets; the natural test set has a propagation tail, and EqR, the slowest starter (12.9 % exact at step 1), is the best finisher.
13. **Overlap.** The decimating maps solve nearly the same puzzles: EqR | X0 Jaccard .917 at D64, EqR | CGAR .910, CGAR | X0 .907; alphaXiv's failures fall 60–62 % inside the others'. The field's frontier is one solved set with a 1.5-point spread.

### 1.3 What the field's ledger says in one sentence each

EMA and the digit orbit and the mixer carry the accuracy; ACT carries anti-memorization and a verifier; RI/NI carry the selector; depth carries +1 pp per doubling for free; the curriculum carries cold at the selector's expense; the D16 headline, the noise-.5 eval and the best-test-checkpoint selection are protocol conventions that hide 6–10 points and a non-stationary trajectory; and none of the four models is a contractive fixed point in its latent while all four are readout-stable.

## 3. Natives and the field together, through one lens (the corpus tables)

`corpus_lens_20260907.txt` carries one row per grid for 38 grids: 19 natives (d16 → d128, canvas and native, every regime), 6 field-loop grids of ours (X0, X1, X2, A0–A2), 5 DEC arms, the 4 ported public checkpoints through our evaluator, and the Mac's 5 field rows in their own code (HRM among them). Per grid: the R1 class from its D64 records (g50, yield), first-exact, the C1 dynamics on strat-128 at t64 (commitment at step 1, confidently-wrong at step 1 → 64 on failures, monotone solves, flips-to-wrong per cell, syndrome oscillation), the C2 decimation quality at stalls (committed / wrong-among-committed / peeling contradiction at τ .9), and the C3 calibration at stalls.

**Law 1 — the loop sets the decoder class, and the class letter is a two-axis object.** Every native from 78k to 3.0M parameters reads SOFT on the threshold-and-yield axis (g50 25.7–38 givens inside the tested range; search yields 0–37.6 %) AND soft on the dynamics axis (3–13 % of cells committed at step 1, 0 % confidently wrong at step 1 rising to 0–46 % by step 64, monotone solves 0–18 %, first-exact median 7–17). Every field-loop grid, every DEC arm and every public TRM-class model commits 91–99 % of cells at step 1 on solved AND unsolved puzzles, is 43–72 % confidently wrong at step 1 on its failures, solves monotonically 39–77 % of the time, first-exact median 1–3, and contradicts peeling at 100 % of its stalls. The two axes dissociate exactly on the two field-loop cells whose operator or orbit was removed: X2 (our group mixer inside the TRM loop) and X1 (the field cell without the digit orbit, memorized) read SOFT by threshold and yield (g50 26.1 / 29.1, yields 35 / 17 %) while their dynamics are the field's (91 / 98 % committed at step 1). The operator and the orbit set the accuracy; the loop sets the regime. HRM sits in between on both axes (MIXED: g50 24.0, yield 54; commit 89 / 87, monotone 20, revising the most).

**Law 2 — the calibrated-commitment gap is class-intrinsic, and the natives are the better calibrated.** At stalls, the five most confident cells are right 63–83 % of the time on the natives (one exception at 51) at mean confidence .85–1.00, versus 41–62 % on every field-loop and DEC grid and 45–54 % on the four public models at confidence 1.000; readout entropy at step 1 .44–.59 versus .007–.04. The DEC inherits the field's gap unchanged (54–62 %). The confidence a decimating decoder reports at a stall is a decision, not a probability; 100 % of stalls contradict peeling from the committed cells on every decimating grid measured (18 grids). This is the mechanism the commit head was designed to move, and no model in the field has moved it.

**Law 3 — the selector law, now on 40 grids.** Verification-free residual selection recovers ≥ .98 of the verifier's coverage where RI/NI training cleaned the attractors (our RI+FPA natives ≤ .4 % spurious; the field's EqR 0.0; our A2 0.0; the DEC's A5 .04) and fails by seed on RI-free maps of either cell class (X0 seed 0 7.8 % / seed 1 (A0) 38 %; the DEC seed 0 4.9 % / seed 1 68.6 %; alphaXiv 2.6; CGAR 31; HRM 12–15; the memorized native P1 7.1; the carried native R1 36). The verifier's coverage is ≥ 97.5 on every field-loop map and 99.5–99.9 on the DEC. Verification-free selection is a property of training, not of the cell.

**Law 4 — the memorization / trajectory law holds in every regime measured.** Our natives memorize the 1k corpus under wd 1e-4 by 30–40k steps (H-49), under wd 1.0 at our lr by 48–56k (the rate law); the field cell without the digit orbit by 10k; the DEC, exactly equivariant, at the field's rate with peaks at 10–42k and finals 12–23 points under them; EqR's released model sits +6.9 pp above its test rate on its own corpus; alphaXiv's later grid is 3.85 pp under its earlier one. Selection is load-bearing in every regime, including the field's, and the selection instrument's resolution (64 puzzles: 1.6 pp) set Night A's 5-point seed spread. Position augmentation is the equivariant cell's only orbit.

**Law 5 — the depth law.** Decimating decoders gain about +1 pp per doubling of the outer-step budget from D16 to D256 with zero regressions (three public models × four depth rows; the DEC +0.5 to +1.1 at D128 with less left to gain); soft natives gain nothing past their stall (+1.6 at 256 steps, sportC2). Depth is propagation only for a decoder that commits.

**Law 6 — exact symmetry replaces the orbit at parity.** A3 − X1 = +68.8 pp on identical puzzles; A4 − A3 inside the corrected floor; SE-RRM's concurrent result with attention over symbols and digit augmentation confirms the class; the field's own models flip the solve status of 24–50 % of puzzles under relabeling (the Mac's orbit row) — the defect an equivariant cell has at zero by construction.

**Law 7 — init-invariance is trainable and recipe-dependent.** Init radius at D16 (exact at ε = 3 as a fraction of cold): EqR .99, alphaXiv .94, CGAR .80, HRM ≈ 0 (the Mac); the DEC's b1 ≈ cold on four of five arms; RI makes the field cell init-invariant (A2) and our natives too (H-37); the curriculum and HRM's training make the trained start load-bearing.

**Compute (the honest column).** The DEC-w384's 70 GMAC per outer step is 4.6× the TRM-MLP's 15.2; at D128 the DEC's 98.6 costs 9.0 TMAC per puzzle where EqR's 95.0 costs 1.9; the accuracy-per-MAC curve has no DEC point at or below the field's compute yet (w192 = 1.34× is the missing point).

## 5. The champion night, designed from the measurements (summary; the registration draft has the arms, bands, rules and walls)

The score-maximizing recipe is measured: the DEC at w384 in the field regime with FPA k1 and RI σ1 (A5 = B0 by rule), no digit augmentation, EMA headline, val-selected on a 512-puzzle monitor with the earliest tie, evaluated at D64 and D128 with the D256 row (each doubling +0.5 pp on this cell), and reported with the verification-free residual column at the verifier's level.
